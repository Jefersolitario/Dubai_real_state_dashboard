# Industry-standard accuracy metrics, thresholds and published error rates for residential AVMs (state of knowledge: 15 November 2026)

- Zillow's own accuracy pages returned HTTP 403/503 to every fetch, so Zillow figures are taken from secondary pages that quote Zillow and that were opened. This is flagged wherever it applies.
- "MdAPE" = median absolute percentage error (Zillow and Redfin call it "median error rate"; Clear Capital calls it MdAE). "PPE10" = share of estimates within 10% of the benchmark (also written P10, PE(10), "percentage within 10%").
- The reader's model, for orientation only (not researched): out-of-sample MdAPE about 4.1% (resale about 6.0%, off-plan about 3.4%), 79% within 10%, 94% within 20%, COD about 6.8, no listing prices used.

## 1. Which accuracy metrics are standard for AVMs, and who defines them?

### Takeaway
There is no single global standard-setter. Three families of metrics coexist: (a) percentage-error statistics against a sale-price benchmark used by vendors, lenders and testers (median/mean absolute error, PPE10/PPE20, bias, hit rate, forecast standard deviation and confidence scores), documented by the European AVM Alliance, Freddie Mac, MISMO, AVMetrics and vendor glossaries; (b) assessor ratio-study statistics (median ratio, COD, PRD, PRB, and from 2026 the VEI) defined by the IAAO; and (c) generic machine-learning metrics (MAPE, RMSE, R2) used in academic papers. MdAPE is the usual consumer-facing headline; lending-grade practice leans on PPE10/PPE20, bias, coverage and a calibrated confidence measure.

### Cited Findings

#### Percentage-error metrics (vendor / lender / tester family)
- The European AVM Alliance (EAA) splits accuracy into two dimensions: "Bias (typically quantified by the Average Error or preferably by the Median Error)" and "Dispersion (typically quantified by the Standard Deviation, or the Average Absolute Error, or the percentages of results within 5%, 10% etc of the Benchmark Value)". PDF dated 19 Nov 2021. — [EAA Glossary of Terms & Definitions](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Glossary_v23.pdf)
- EAA defines the error with the benchmark in the denominator: "The relative difference between a valuation result, e.g. from an AVM, and the Benchmark Value, expressed as a percentage of the Benchmark Value (not of the AVM): (AVM - BV) / BV". — [EAA Glossary](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Glossary_v23.pdf)
- EAA on "Percentage within 10%, 15%, 20% etc": "The percentage of results ... with an Error <= 10%, 15% or 20% respectively, regardless of its + or - sign", and "This is the most often used measure of AVM Accuracy, capturing the Dispersion of the Errors in perhaps a more intuitive way for the layman user than the Standard Deviation". — [EAA Glossary](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Glossary_v23.pdf)
- EAA standards (3rd edition, effective 1 March 2022): "the most often quoted measure of Dispersion is the percentage of results with an Error within 20% of the Benchmark Value, because granting a 10% margin to both the result of the Statistical Valuation Method and to the Benchmark Value, the two may differ by up to 20% without necessarily leading to the conclusion that either of them must be inaccurate." Margins "should be set to ... no less than 10%". — [European Standards for Statistical Valuation Methods for Residential Properties, 3rd ed., s.5.2](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)
- Clear Capital's testing glossary (31 May 2019) defines: hit rate = "The number of benchmark addresses the AVM was able to predict on"; MAE = mean of absolute percent variances between AVM and benchmark; MdAE = the median version, which "hides the times the AVM was really far off; i.e. the outliers" and is "the most common measure for consumer-grade (not professional/lending-grade) AVMs"; PPE10 (or P10) = "The percentage of time the AVM is within 10 percent of the benchmark", which does not capture predictions that are very far off; FSD: "The lower the FSD, the better quality/accuracy of the AVM". — [Clear Capital, AVM Testing: A Short Glossary](https://www.clearcapital.com/blog-avm-testing-glossary/)
- HouseCanary's 2022 standards white paper (PDF created 22 Jul 2022) defines the reporting set as mean percent error (MPE, bias), mean absolute percent error (MAPE), median percent error (MdPE), median absolute percent error (MdAPE), and "PPE 5, PPE 10 and PPE 20 ... the percentage of the AVM estimates that fall within the threshold error"; it recommends reporting both mean-based and percentile-based statistics because mean-based ones "are also vulnerable to outliers". — [HouseCanary, Standards for Automated Property Valuation](https://www.housecanary.com/images/65b19f5d2af745c8a24e1f7c_AE-AVM_Standards_Whitepaper_min-1.pdf)
- AVMetrics (independent US AVM tester), comment letter to the six agencies dated 21 Aug 2023: "Mean Absolute Error and P10 seem to have a clear advantage over all other options. And, because P10 is much more widely adopted and it is simple and easy to convey, we recommend the adoption of P10 as a standardized 'Universal Confidence Score' for AVMs." It notes FSD "was proposed early on" as the universal metric but is hard for users to understand. — [AVMetrics comment letter, RIN 2590-AA62](https://fhfa.gov/sites/default/files/rulemaking_comments/Attachments/16315/14_AVMetrics%20LLC.pdf)
- Consumer portals headline the median: Redfin states "a median error rate of 1.94% for on-market homes" and explains it as the estimate being within that margin of the sale price half of the time, alongside table columns for share within 5%, 10% and 20%. — [Redfin Estimate page, "Last updated 11/14/26"](https://www.redfin.com/redfin-estimate)
- HouseCanary's public accuracy page reports, per benchmark: MdAPE, share within 5% / 10% / 20%, signed median error, number of sales tested and coverage. — [HouseCanary accuracy page, updated 11 Nov 2026](https://www.housecanary.com/accuracy/)
- In Norway, banks and credit institutions report AVMs "by the share of predictions within +/-20% and +/-10% of true values" (abstract of a 2022 Journal of Property Research paper). — [Hjort, Pensar, Scheel & Sommervoll (2022), JPR 39(4):338-364](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:jpropr:v:39:y:2022:i:4:p:338-364)

#### Forecast standard deviation (FSD), confidence scores, hit rate / coverage
- Freddie Mac (Douglas Gordon, director of collateral modeling; article copyright 2005, PDF created 22 Mar 2005): "FSD is an AVM value's expected (forecasted) proportional standard deviation around actual subsequent sales price for the given property value estimate", and "if FSD for property estimates are 10%, about 68% of actual sale prices will fall within +10% of the AVM values." — [Freddie Mac, Metrics Matter](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheets/dougwhitepaper_metricsmatter.pdf)
- EAA: FSD is "The Standard Deviation of the Error distribution predicted for a set of AVM results with a given Confidence Level"; a Confidence Level is "directly translatable into a Forecast Standard Deviation. It is typically given on the EAA's 0 to 7 proprietary scale"; "a Value Range of +/-1FSD is expected to include the Market Value with approximately 68% confidence; a Value Range of +/-2FSD ... approximately 96%". — [EAA Glossary](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Glossary_v23.pdf)
- Clear Capital: "The AVM confidence score is typically based on the standard deviation of the valuation prediction", and vendors' FSD methods are proprietary so scores are not standardized across vendors. — [Clear Capital glossary](https://www.clearcapital.com/blog-avm-testing-glossary/)
- IAAO (2018): "Various confidence score methods may be found in vendor applications ... However, parameters and methodologies for these techniques have not been standardized and it may be difficult to compare similarly named measures produced by different vendor-provided models. This standard takes no position on the validity or usefulness of such guidance." — [IAAO Standard on Automated Valuation Models, revised July 2018](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- MISMO (US mortgage-industry standards body) published an AVM "Common Confidence Score" standard that reached Recommendation status ("the final step for a MISMO standard") on 21 Apr 2026; implementers named: Clear Capital, Consolidated Analytics, Cotality, ICE, Quantarium and Veros Real Estate Solutions. — [MISMO news release, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.mismo.org/about-MISMO/news/2026/04/21/mismo-common-confidence-score-reaches-recommendation-status-following-implementation-by-leading-avm-providers)
- AVMetrics describes that score as "the estimated probability that a valuation falls within plus or minus ten percent of the property's actual market value" and quotes MISMO's guidance: "85% of the AVM values with a Common Confidence Score of .85 will be within 10% of the market value." — [AVMetrics blog, 4 Apr 2026 (updated 6 Apr 2026)](https://www.avmetrics.net/2026/04/04/avm-confidence-score-calibration/)
- MISMO's product page lists the Common Confidence Score Standard and Guidance (Recommendation, 21 Apr 2026) and a "Testing Guidance and Best Practices" document announced 29 Sep 2026, available to members or under licence. — [MISMO product page](https://www.mismo.org/standards-resources/mismo-product/automated-valuation-model-common-confidence-score-standard-and-guidance)
- EAA: "Performance ... comprises Coverage, Accuracy and the reliability of any Confidence Measures", and "Coverage can be high at the expense of Accuracy ... and vice versa ... the two aspects of Coverage and Accuracy cannot be considered in isolation from each other." Hit Rate = cases producing a valuation / cases meeting input requirements; Success Rate and Pass Rate additionally account for user output rules. — [EAA Standards 3rd ed., ch.5](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)
- IAAO glossary: "Hit Rates - The number of times the model or cascading models return a useable value estimate. Not a measure of how well the models fit the data". — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018)](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)

#### Assessor ratio-study statistics (IAAO family)
- COD: "The most generally useful measure of variability or uniformity is the COD. The COD measures the average percentage deviation of the ratios from the median ratio": subtract the median from each ratio, take absolute values, sum, divide by the number of ratios, divide by the median, multiply by 100. Ratio = appraised (model) value / sale price. — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies, approved April 2013, s.5.4.1](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)
- PRD = mean ratio divided by the weighted mean ratio; "Price-related differentials above 1.03 tend to indicate assessment regressivity; price-related differentials below 0.98 tend to indicate assessment progressivity." — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies, exposure draft May 2026, glossary](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)
- PRB: "obtained by regressing percentage deviations from the median ratio on percentage changes in a value proxy ... Indicates the percentage by which assessment ratios change whenever values are doubled or halved. For example, a PRB of -0.03 means assessment levels decrease by 3 percent when value doubles." — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026, glossary](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)
- The IAAO AVM standard tells AVM developers to use these same statistics: "Sales based ratio studies are among the most objective methods for testing the performance and quality of any valuation system"; "the COD is recommended as the variability statistic of choice"; "Standards for interpreting CODs are contained in the Standard on Ratio Studies"; "The median ratio, considered in conjunction with its confidence interval, and distribution of ratios using quantiles and the COD should be used to evaluate whether AVM results attain market value standards." — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018), s.7.3](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- The May 2026 IAAO draft adds MAPE and MdAPE to its list of "other measures of variability" ("median absolute percentage error (MdAPE): Measure of accuracy of predictive models which is less susceptible to outliers due to its use of the median") and states: "Note that the typical percentage error is not the COD but is expressed by the median absolute percentage error statistic." It also notes "more than half the ratios fall within one COD of the median" and "in a normal distribution, the COV is approximately 1.25 times the COD." — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026, s.8.1](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)
- The same draft makes a new Vertical Equity Indicator (VEI) the primary vertical-equity test, with PRB, the Modified Kakwani Index and PRD as supplements. — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026, s.8.2](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)

#### Academic machine-learning family
- Steurer and Hill list the metrics "more commonly used" in the AVM literature as "Mean Prediction Error (MPE), Mean Absolute Prediction Error (MAPE), Coefficient of Dispersion (COD), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and R2", and note that definitions vary between papers (for example COD around the median versus the mean, and errors with the actual versus the predicted price in the denominator). — [Steurer & Hill, Graz Economics Papers 2020-02](https://unipub.uni-graz.at/obvugrveroeff/download/pdf/9606995?originalFilename=true)

### Inferences
- Name mapping the writer can rely on: PPE10 = P10 = PE(10) = "percentage within 10%" = 100% minus Steurer-Hill's PER(10); MdAPE = MdAE (in %) = Zillow/Redfin "median error rate". All are shares or medians of |estimate - price| / price.
- COD is a mean-based statistic (average absolute deviation around the median ratio), so with a median ratio near 1 it behaves like a MAPE, not like an MdAPE. Under a normal error distribution COD would be about 1.18 times MdAPE (computed: 0.798 sigma versus 0.674 sigma). The reader's COD 6.8 against MdAPE 4.1 is a ratio of about 1.66, which indicates heavier tails than a normal distribution (or a mixture of a tight and a loose segment).
- "Who defines them" in one line: IAAO defines COD/PRD/PRB/median ratio (and VEI); Freddie Mac popularised FSD and FSD-based confidence tiers; EAA defines error, bias, dispersion, percentage-within-X, hit/success/pass rate and confidence level for Europe; MISMO now defines a common confidence score tied to the plus/minus 10% band; MdAPE/MAPE/PPE have no single owner and are vendor and tester conventions.

## 2. What numeric thresholds or acceptable ranges exist, and does any regulator set a numeric accuracy threshold?

### Takeaway
Numeric ranges exist only in voluntary professional or industry documents: the IAAO ratio-study standards (median ratio 0.90-1.10; COD 5.0-10.0 for newer or homogeneous residential including condominiums and 5.0-15.0 for older or heterogeneous areas; PRD 0.98-1.03; PRB within +/-0.05, unacceptable beyond +/-0.10), Freddie Mac's HVE confidence tiers (high = FSD of 13% or less, medium = 13-20%, low = above 20%), and a vendor proposal (HouseCanary: PPE10 of at least 80% list-informed and at least 60% pre-listing). No regulator or valuation body reviewed sets a numeric accuracy threshold: the 2024 US six-agency rule, the EBA guidelines, the European AVM Alliance standards and RICS impose process, testing, confidence-measure and reporting requirements only.

### Cited Findings

#### IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (approved April 2013; still the adopted version found)
- Table 1-3, "Ratio Study Uniformity Standards indicating acceptable general quality", COD ranges: single-family residential (including residential condominiums), newer or more homogeneous areas 5.0 to 10.0; single-family residential, older or more heterogeneous areas 5.0 to 15.0; other residential (rural, seasonal, recreational, manufactured housing, 2-4 unit family housing) 5.0 to 20.0; income-producing properties, larger areas with large samples 5.0 to 15.0, smaller areas with smaller samples 5.0 to 20.0; vacant land 5.0 to 25.0. — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (2013), Table 1-3](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)
- Section 9.2.2: "The COD for single-family homes and condominiums in older or more heterogeneous areas should be between 5.0 and 15.0. In areas of newer or fairly similar residences, it should be between 5.0 and 10.0." — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (2013)](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)
- Table footnotes: "Appraisal level for each type of property shown should be between 0.90 and 1.10, unless stricter local standards are required"; "PRD's for each type of property should be between 0.98 and 1.03 to demonstrate vertical equity. PRD standards are not absolute and may be less meaningful when samples are small or when wide variation in prices exist"; "CODs lower than 5.0 may indicate sales chasing or non-representative samples." — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (2013), Table 1-3](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)
- Level: "While the theoretically desired level of appraisal is 1.00, an appraisal level between 0.90 and 1.10 is considered acceptable for any class of property. However, each class of property must be within 5 percent of the overall level of appraisal of the jurisdiction". — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (2013), s.9.1](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)
- PRB: "As a general matter, the PRB coefficient should fall between -0.05 and 0.05 ... PRBs for which 95% confidence intervals fall outside the range of -0.10 to 0.10 indicate unacceptable vertical inequities." PRD is not centred on 1.00 because of "an inherent upward bias in the arithmetic mean". — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (2013), s.9.2.7](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)
- Table 2-3 (oversight-agency part) gives 5.0 to 10.0 for residential improved property (single-family dwellings, condominiums, manufactured housing, 2-4 family units) in "very large jurisdictions/densely populated/newer properties/active markets", 5.0 to 15.0 for large to mid-sized jurisdictions, 5.0 to 20.0 for rural or small jurisdictions; and states "The COD performance recommendations are based upon representative and adequate sample sizes, with outliers trimmed and a 95% level of confidence." — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (2013), Table 2-3](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)
- Caveats in the standard: "CODs less than 5 percent should be considered unusual and possibly indicative of nonrepresentative samples or the selective reappraisal of sold parcels"; the COD standards "are intended to apply to ratio studies based on sales"; they "may not be applicable to properties in unique, depressed, or rapidly changing markets"; and IAAO standards "are advisory in nature and the use of, or compliance with, such standards is voluntary." — [IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies (2013)](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)

#### IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies, revision in progress (exposure draft dated May 2026)
- Status: the draft "replaces the 2013 Standard on Ratio Studies and is a complete revision". IAAO's news page says "The 2013 version remains the recommended guidance" until the Board approves a final version; IAAO's governing-documents page (opened 15 Nov 2026) says the standard is "temporarily unavailable while revisions are being finalized" and that the final version "will be reposted as soon as it is available". — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf); [IAAO news: ratio studies draft](https://www.iaao.org/news/iaao-releases-ratio-studies-draft/); [IAAO ratio studies exposure draft page](https://www.iaao.org/about/board-of-directors/governing-documents/ratio-studies-exposure-draft/)
- Draft Table 7 COD ranges: Residential Improved 5.0 to 15.0; "Newer or more homogeneous market areas such as condominiums" 4.0 to 10.0; rural, seasonally occupied housing or manufactured homes 10.0 to 20.0; Multi-Unit Apartments 7.0 to 20.0 (rural 10.0 to 25.0); commercial/industrial 10.0 to 25.0; vacant land 10.0 to 20.0. Footnotes: valuation level 0.90 to 1.10; "The acceptable IAAO standard range for the VEI is -10% to 10%". — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026, Table 7](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)
- Draft text keeps PRB ("PRBs of +/-.05 tend to indicate good equity"; glossary: "The PRB should range between -0.05 and +0.05. PRBs outside the range of -0.10 to +0.10 are considered unacceptable") and PRD ("Ratios of 0.98 to 1.03 can indicate good equity, although the measure is highly influenced by value and ratio outliers") as supplementary measures. — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)
- Draft adds model overfitting as a reason to distrust very low CODs: CODs below the table minimum "should be treated as a diagnostic warning. Such results may indicate issues such as sales chasing (selective reappraisal of sold properties), non-representative sales samples, or model overfitting." It also adds separate, wider COD ranges for distressed or blighted (residential 10.0 to 25.0) and emerging (10.0 to 35.0) market areas. — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026, s.11.2.5 and Table 5](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)

#### IAAO Standard on Automated Valuation Models (revised, approved July 2018)
- Reproduces the 2013 COD table as "Table 2. Ratio Study Performance Standards" (5.0 to 10.0 for single-family residential including residential condominiums in newer or more homogeneous areas; 5.0 to 15.0 older or more heterogeneous), with level 0.90-1.10, PRD 0.98-1.03, PRB +/-0.05 and significant inequity beyond +/-0.10. — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018), Appendix G Table 2](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- "An AVM should be tested to determine if it meets required accuracy and uniformity standards before initial use and after implementation ... the AVM also should be tested using sale prices that were not used in the calibration process (e.g., holdout sample or other cross-validation techniques)." — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018), s.3.4.8](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- Worked example of a level test: "say the standard is that the level of the value estimate indicated by model results should be within 10% of market value. This standard should be considered met if a 90% two-tailed confidence interval around the median sales ratio overlaps the range of 90% to 110% of market value." — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018), s.7.3.3](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- "Standards do not exist for goodness-of-fit statistics (such as the coefficient of determination)". — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018)](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)

#### Freddie Mac Home Value Explorer (HVE) confidence tiers
- "Using HVE as an example, a high confidence score includes FSDs of 13% or less, while a medium score includes FSDs between 13% and 20%, and a low confidence score includes FSDs greater than 20%." (Published 2005; these are product tiers, not a regulatory threshold.) — [Freddie Mac, Metrics Matter](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheets/dougwhitepaper_metricsmatter.pdf)
- Validation test proposed in the same paper: "Are about 68% of the AVM values tested within 1 standard deviation (reported as the model's FSD) of the actual price?"; for HVE, "The lender can verify whether at least 68% of the test values fall within +13% of the high confidence values"; a 1.28-FSD interval should contain about 80% and a 1.65-FSD interval about 90%. "The best way to validate confidence score or FSD is by performing an out-of-sample test". — [Freddie Mac, Metrics Matter](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheets/dougwhitepaper_metricsmatter.pdf)

#### European AVM Alliance standards (3rd edition, effective 1 March 2022)
- The standards "set the minimum requirements for Statistical Valuation Methods in Europe"; the requirements are about testing and reporting, not a pass mark: "Scrutiny procedures must deploy strictly out-of-sample and Blind Tests"; bulk-test reports "should contain tables and figures, such as a histogram of the distribution of Error ... histogram bins span no more than 5% ranges"; "For each segment [property type, geographic area, price range], such tables should display at a minimum the number of cases and the key indicators of Accuracy, i.e., the percentage of results within 10%, 15% and 20% of the Benchmark Value." — [EAA Standards 3rd ed., s.4.3 and s.5.5](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)
- "A Confidence Measure must be predictive. For that to be the case it must be reliably translatable into a Forecast Standard Deviation." — [EAA Standards 3rd ed., s.5.3](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)
- No minimum accuracy level was found in the performance chapters (4-5) or by keyword search of the document; the only number is the instruction that error margins be "no less than 10%". — [EAA Standards 3rd ed.](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)

#### RICS
- RICS's AVM page lists two documents only: an insight paper (published 1 April 2022) and the RICS AVM Roadmap (19 July 2021); the page text sets no numeric accuracy threshold, and the insight paper (opened in full) contains none. The roadmap itself was not opened. — [RICS, Automated valuation models](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/valuation-standards/automated-valuation-models)
- The insight paper proposes a due-diligence framework of questions, including "What level of confidence for an output is available, and how is that defined and calculated?", "Is the AVM free from risk around bias and drift", "Is the AVM stress tested against periods of market volatility", and what "methodologies [are] used to calibrate and back-test against external arms-length benchmarks"; it concludes "A separate standalone standard for AVMs and automation per se may not be the best approach." — [RICS insight paper, AVMs: implications for the profession and their clients, April 2022](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/april_2022_automated_valuation_models_insight.pdf)
- The Red Book Global Standards (2025 edition, effective 31 January 2025) "amended existing mandatory (PS1) standards around AVMs", "incorporated new standards covering automation and AI" and split out a new VPS 5 (Valuation models); the RICS page states no accuracy threshold. — [RICS Red Book Global Standards page](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/valuation-standards/red-book/red-book-global)

#### EBA Guidelines on loan origination and monitoring (EBA/GL/2020/06, 29 May 2020; apply from 30 June 2021)
- Para 237: "Institutions should ensure that the advanced statistical models used are: a. property and location specific at a sufficient level of granularity (e.g. postcode for immovable property collateral); b. valid and accurate, and subject to robust and regular backtesting against the actual observed transaction prices; c. based on a sufficiently large and representative sample, based on observed transaction prices; d. based on up-to-date data of high quality." No numeric accuracy level is given. — [EBA/GL/2020/06 Final Report, s.7.4](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library/Publications/Guidelines/2020/Guidelines%20on%20loan%20origination%20and%20monitoring/884283/EBA%20GL%202020%2006%20Final%20Report%20on%20GL%20on%20loan%20origination%20and%20monitoring.pdf)
- Para 210: at origination, residential property in "well-developed and mature property markets" may be valued by a desktop valuation "supported by advanced statistical models ... including a confidence measure to indicate the robustness of the value proposal"; "If the confidence measure in the supporting advanced statistical model indicates low robustness ... the valuer should choose a valuation method other than desktop valuation." Para 236 requires policies to account for the models' "proven track record ... and models' uncertainty." — [EBA/GL/2020/06 Final Report](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library/Publications/Guidelines/2020/Guidelines%20on%20loan%20origination%20and%20monitoring/884283/EBA%20GL%202020%2006%20Final%20Report%20on%20GL%20on%20loan%20origination%20and%20monitoring.pdf)

#### US interagency final rule, Quality Control Standards for Automated Valuation Models
- Published in the Federal Register on 7 August 2024 (89 FR 64538) by the OCC, Federal Reserve Board, FDIC, NCUA, CFPB and FHFA; "This final rule is effective October 1, 2025." Institutions must adopt "policies, practices, procedures, and control systems" so that AVMs used for mortgages on a consumer's principal dwelling adhere to quality control standards designed to "ensure a high level of confidence in the estimates produced by AVMs; protect against the manipulation of data; seek to avoid conflicts of interest; require random sample testing and reviews; and comply with applicable nondiscrimination laws." — [Federal Register, 7 Aug 2024, final rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-07/pdf/2024-16197.pdf)
- No numeric threshold: the proposed rule "would not have set specific requirements for how institutions are to structure these policies, practices, procedures, and control systems", and the final rule keeps that design ("The proposed and now adopted approach will allow mortgage originators and secondary market issuers the flexibility to set their quality control standards for covered AVMs as appropriate based on the size, complexity, and risk profile of their institution"). The agencies "determined that prescriptive rules would pose a challenge due to the inherent complexity of AVMs and their use cases", noted "the statute does not require the agencies to set prescriptive standards for AVMs", and concluded that a policies-and-procedures rule "will more effectively carry out the purposes of section 1125 than a more prescriptive rule". One commenter had "suggested that AVMs need standardized confidence scores and standardized reporting formats"; the final rule contains neither. — [Federal Register, 7 Aug 2024, final rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-07/pdf/2024-16197.pdf)
- AVMetrics' view of the consequence (comment letter, 21 Aug 2023): "With the proposed non-prescriptive guidelines, many lenders will not know how to evaluate" their AVM, and "many will do the absolute minimum". — [AVMetrics comment letter](https://fhfa.gov/sites/default/files/rulemaking_comments/Attachments/16315/14_AVMetrics%20LLC.pdf)

#### Vendor-proposed numeric minima (not a regulation)
- HouseCanary (2022) proposes "minimum requirements for AVM accuracy" for an AVM to replace an appraisal: "MPE is between -2% and 2%"; "MdPE is between -2% and 2%"; "Purchase PPE10 greater than or equal to 80%"; "Prelist PPE10 greater than or equal to 60%", derived from appraisal-error data from Fannie Mae ("an AVM should equal or surpass the performance of traditional appraisals"). The purchase benchmark "allows the AVM to use the list price as an anchor"; the prelist benchmark is the AVM value before the property was listed. — [HouseCanary, Standards for Automated Property Valuation (2022)](https://www.housecanary.com/images/65b19f5d2af745c8a24e1f7c_AE-AVM_Standards_Whitepaper_min-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Against IAAO ranges, a COD of about 6.8 sits inside the 5.0-10.0 band for newer or homogeneous residential including condominiums (2013 standard and 2018 AVM standard) and inside the draft 4.0-10.0 band for "condominiums". Caveats the writer should carry: the IAAO ranges are for assessor ratio studies (assessed value over sale price, values fixed before the sale), the recommended CODs assume outliers have been trimmed, and the standards are advisory.
- Against HouseCanary's proposed minima, 79% within 10% with no listing price is well above the "prelist" 60% bar and just under the list-informed "purchase" 80% bar. Signed bias (mean and median percentage error within +/-2%) would need to be reported to complete that comparison.
- Translating the reader's figures into an implied FSD under a normal-error assumption (my arithmetic): 79% within 10% implies sigma of about 8.0%; 94% within 20% implies about 10.6%; MdAPE 4.1% implies about 6.1%; COD 6.8 implies about 8.5%. All are below the 13% that defines HVE's high-confidence tier. For reference, an FSD of exactly 13% corresponds to about 56% within 10%, 88% within 20% and an MdAPE of about 8.8%; an FSD of 20% to about 38% within 10% and an MdAPE of about 13.5%.
- The rising implied sigma from centre (6.1%) to tail (10.6%) is another sign of fat tails: the tail, not the median, is where the model is weakest relative to its headline.
- Bottom line for "does any regulator set a number?": among the US rule, EBA, EAA and RICS, none does. Numbers come from IAAO (voluntary, assessor-oriented), Freddie Mac's product tiers, MISMO's definition of a confidence score around the 10% band, and vendor proposals.

## 3. What error rates do leading AVM providers currently publish, and why are on-market figures much lower?

### Takeaway

## 4. What do independent testers and peer-reviewed studies report, especially for apartments and for Dubai / the Gulf?

### Takeaway
Independent testers (AVMetrics) test every commercial US AVM on pre-listing values but do not publish model-level results. Peer-reviewed out-of-sample studies of apartment-heavy markets report MdAPE of roughly 5-11% and 37-47% within 10% for generic models, with the best urban Scandinavian and Korean results around 5.4-5.5%; one presentation of the academic evidence puts irreducible "transaction noise" at a standard deviation of about 7%. Published work on Dubai Land Department data is thin and weak: the credible figures are a MAPE of 11% (700,000+ transactions, random forest) and MAPEs of 25-36% for standard learners on 247,517 one-bedroom sales.

### Cited Findings

#### Independent testers
- AVMetrics says it tests "EVERY commercially available AVM using hundreds of thousands of transactions each month" and summarises results in a proprietary Model Preference Table; the homepage publishes no accuracy numbers. — [AVMetrics](https://www.avmetrics.net/)
- "In Q4 [2024], AVMetrics independently tested 25 models"; only 14 ranked first in any county; "At least 11 AVMs shouldn't be anyone's first choice ANYWHERE". Its testing uses "a national dataset of 500,000 to 700,000+ records per quarter, covering 1,700 counties". No PPE10 or error values are published in the blog. — [AVMetrics blog index](https://www.avmetrics.net/blog); [AVMetrics blog, 4 Apr 2026](https://www.avmetrics.net/2026/04/04/avm-confidence-score-calibration/)
- AVMetrics completed a move to pre-listing ("predictive") testing, announced 4 May 2026, on the grounds that list-informed tests overstate accuracy. — [AVMetrics blog, 4 May 2026](https://www.avmetrics.net/2026/05/04/predictive-testing-methodology-replaces-legacy-avm-testing/)

#### Peer-reviewed and academic out-of-sample results
- Oslo, Norway: an AVM combining four ensemble learners with a repeat-sales method, tested out of sample on 1,979 dwellings sold in Q1 2018, reached a "median absolute percentage error of 5.4%" and "more than 96% of the dwellings are estimated within 20%" of the sale price; against local estate agents, "in stable market phases, the machine falls short of human capability". — [Birkeland, D'Silva, Füss & Oust (2021), International Real Estate Review 24(2):139-183](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ire:issued:v:24:n:02:2021:p:139-183)
- Norway, 126,719 transactions (2013-2015), gradient boosted trees: changing the loss function to match the AVM's actual success measure raised the share within +/-20% from 89.4% to 90.0%, and a weighted average of models reached 90.4%; gains were largest in lower price segments. — [Hjort, Pensar, Scheel & Sommervoll (2022), Journal of Property Research 39(4)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:jpropr:v:39:y:2022:i:4:p:338-364)
- Gangnam (Seoul) apartments, transactions 2006-2017: random forest "around 5.5%" average percentage deviation versus "almost 20%" for an OLS hedonic model; 72% of random-forest predictions within 5% of the actual price versus about 17.5% for OLS. — [Hong, Choi & Kim (2020), International Journal of Strategic Property Management 24(3):140-152](https://journals.vilniustech.lt/index.php/IJSPM/article/view/11544)
- Germany, 1,212,546 residential properties (38% condominiums) valued 2014-2020, benchmark = appraiser-based market values from a banking group (not sale prices), test year 2020. XGBoost: MAPE 14.65%, MdAPE 10.84%, PE(10) 46.65%, PE(20) 77.86%, R2 0.8995. GAM: MdAPE 12.02%, PE(10) 42.73%. OLS: MdAPE 13.11%, PE(10) 39.37%. Automated sales-comparison method: MdAPE 16.24%, PE(10) 32.67%. — [Stang, Krämer, Nagl & Schäfers (2022), Zeitschrift für Immobilienökonomie](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9294847/)
- Graz, Austria, apartments, 5,599 transactions 2014-2017, few features, explicitly "for illustrative purposes": best model (random forest) MAPE 0.199, MDAPE 0.146, COD 0.200, PER(10) 0.629 (62.9% of predictions off by more than 10%, i.e. about 37% within 10%) and PER(20) 0.380. — [Steurer & Hill, Graz Economics Papers 2020-02, Table A1](https://unipub.uni-graz.at/obvugrveroeff/download/pdf/9606995?originalFilename=true)
- Transaction noise as a floor: "P = MV + TN" (price = market value + transaction noise); "StDev(TN) around 7%" citing Goetzmann (1993) and Francke (2010); "TN cannot be modeled away, [its variance] is a lower bound for model precision". In a Dutch application (about 2,500 sales of 2,000 single-family homes, 2001-2017) the leave-one-out mean absolute log residual fell from 0.137 to 0.105 after adding property and spatial random effects, and to 0.087 for homes with two earlier sales in the training set. — [Francke, "One size fits all? Different applications of AVMs", IAAO presentation, 4 Dec 2024](https://iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/20241204-IAAO-Francke.pdf)

#### Dubai, UAE and the Gulf
- Dubai, DLD unit sales from the Dubai Pulse open-data portal: 247,517 one-bedroom transactions (25 May 2004 to 9 Oct 2023), eight basic features, 80/20 split. MAPE by model: support vector machine 25.10, gradient boosting 25.39, random forest 26.08, linear regression 26.35, KNN 27.50, ANN 27.65, decision tree 35.94; R2 between 0.34 and -0.28. A hybrid "EEMD-SD-SVM" is reported at MAPE 3.33 with R2 0.541. Published 13 Feb 2024. — [Balila & Shabri (2024), Frontiers in Applied Mathematics and Statistics 10](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fams.2024.1327376)
- Dubai, more than 700,000 DLD transaction records with geospatial features: "A Random Forest Regressor achieved the strongest performance", MAPE 11% and MAE of approximately 1,144 AED (unit basis not stated on the record page). Two-page conference paper, ICONI 2025, 14 Dec 2025; years and property types not stated. — [Alhammadi & Ibrahim (2025), University of Birmingham research portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/machine-learningbased-property-valuation-for-dubai-design-impleme/)
- Dubai, master's thesis (Dec 2025): over 100,000 DLD residential transactions combined with macroeconomic indicators, scraped listing attributes and sentiment; linear regression, decision tree, random forest and XGBoost; the abstract reports an R2 of 0.02 for the best model. — [Nasser (2025), RIT thesis](https://repository.rit.edu/theses/12433)
- Dubai, master's thesis (Jan 2022), "Prediction of Dubai Apartment Prices Using Machine Learning": the abstract reports no error metrics. — [Alshamsi (2022), RIT thesis](https://repository.rit.edu/theses/11179)

### Inferences
- Against the academic record, an out-of-sample MdAPE of about 4.1% (6.0% resale) with 79% / 94% within 10% / 20% is at the strong end: it is better than the Oslo AVM (5.4% MdAPE, more than 96% within 20%) on the median, slightly behind it on the 20% band, and far better than the German (10.8% MdAPE, 47% within 10%) and Graz (14.6%, about 37% within 10%) results. The German and Graz studies use few unit-level comparables, and the German benchmark is appraisals, so they are weak comparators.
- The published Dubai studies are not a meaningful bar: the only large-sample DLD result with a plausible design reports a MAPE of 11% (a mean, which is always above the median), and none uses project-level trailing comparables or the unit's previous sale. No peer-reviewed Dubai or Gulf study reporting MdAPE, PPE10 or COD on DLD transactions was found.
- The Balila-Shabri hybrid's MAPE of 3.33 next to an R2 of 0.54, while seven other learners on the same data sit at 25-36, is internally odd; it should not be quoted as evidence that 3% is achievable on DLD data without reading how the decomposition handles the test set.
- Noise floor arithmetic (mine): a transaction-noise standard deviation of 7% corresponds, under normality, to an MdAPE floor of about 4.7% and a mean absolute error floor of about 5.6% for negotiated sales. A resale MdAPE of 6.0% is therefore not far above what such noise alone would produce, and an overall 4.1% is below it, which is only plausible because list-priced off-plan sales (3.4%) have much less negotiation noise. The 7% figure is from Dutch/US housing, so this is indicative only. It supports reporting resale and off-plan separately and suggests limited remaining headroom in the resale segment.

### Gaps
- AVMetrics does not publish model-level PPE10 / MAE results; an American Enterprise Institute Housing Center 2024 evaluation of five AVM providers is cited by AVMetrics but was not located or opened.
- UNVERIFIED (search snippet only; ResearchGate returned 403): Matysiak (2017), "Automated Valuation Models (AVMs): A brave new world?", is reported to say that on US data "on average some 70% of AVM valuations would fall within the +/- 10% bracket", ranging from 20% to 92% by location.
- Not opened (publisher or host blocked): Oust et al. (2023, Journal of Real Estate Research, Norwegian cities), Čeh et al. (2018, Ljubljana apartments, MDPI returned 403), Schulz & Wersing (2021, Aberdeen), a KFUPM study of graph neural networks on Riyadh and Eastern Province data, and an MIT capstone poster on a Dubai valuation model.
- No peer-reviewed study of AVM accuracy for Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Doha or Kuwait reporting MdAPE or PPE10 was found.

## 5. What accuracy do AVM products in Dubai claim?

## 6. What does the literature say about relying on a single metric such as MdAPE?

### Takeaway
The consistent message from the academic literature, the IAAO, the European AVM Alliance, Freddie Mac and independent testers is that no single number is sufficient: MdAPE describes only the centre of the error distribution and hides bias and tails. Recommended practice is a small set covering bias, dispersion at several bands, tail behaviour, coverage and a calibrated uncertainty measure, with confidence intervals and segment breakdowns, tested blind and out of sample.

### Cited Findings

#### Steurer, Hill and Pfeifer
- Published version (Journal of Property Research 38(2):99-129, April 2021, DOI 10.1080/09599916.2020.1858937), per its abstract: the authors collect 48 performance metrics and classify them into seven groups by structure, each focusing on a different aspect of the error distribution; applying each metric to five common AVM models shows that the choice of metric can change the choice of model; they provide a short list of 7 metrics that satisfy a symmetry condition and give a good overall ranking. Full text is paywalled. — [RePEc record for Steurer, Hill & Pfeifer (2021)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jpropr/v38y2021i2p99-129.html)
- Working-paper version (Steurer & Hill, dated 15 Jan 2020), opened in full: "the most popular metrics in the AVM literature can generate misleading results. A different picture emerges when the full set of metrics is considered". Two symmetry conditions are proposed (errors of equal proportional size should count equally whether over or under; actual and predicted values should be treated symmetrically) and "none of the commonly used metrics satisfy both". MAPE, MDAPE, COD and the percentage-error-range metric all fall in the class that satisfies only the first. — [Steurer & Hill, Graz Economics Papers 2020-02](https://unipub.uni-graz.at/obvugrveroeff/download/pdf/9606995?originalFilename=true)
- Empirical illustration (Graz apartments, 5,599 transactions): across 49 metrics, random forest was best on 27, quantile regression with LASSO on 11 and a neural net on 14; on the six metrics most used in the AVM literature (MPE, MAPE, COD, RMSE, MAE, R2) random forest "did not rank first for any". — [Steurer & Hill, Graz Economics Papers 2020-02](https://unipub.uni-graz.at/obvugrveroeff/download/pdf/9606995?originalFilename=true)
- The working paper's recommended short list is four metrics: log median prediction error (LMDPE, central tendency / bias, "median based and hence also robust to outliers"), log root mean square error (LRMSE, dispersion, not robust to outliers), max-min percentage error range (mmPER, share outside a band such as 10%), and the inter-quartile range of the ratios (IQRAT). — [Steurer & Hill, Graz Economics Papers 2020-02, s.6.2](https://unipub.uni-graz.at/obvugrveroeff/download/pdf/9606995?originalFilename=true)
- Definitional trap shown in the paper: with predicted price 1 and actual price 1.11, the error is 11% with the prediction in the denominator but 9.9% with the actual price in the denominator, so the same case is outside or inside a 10% band depending on the formula. — [Steurer & Hill, Graz Economics Papers 2020-02](https://unipub.uni-graz.at/obvugrveroeff/download/pdf/9606995?originalFilename=true)

#### IAAO
- "While ratio study statistics provide valuable guidance as to the overall quality of the AVM value estimates, statistical measures do not specify the accuracy of individual property estimates." — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018), s.7.3](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- The standard asks for a battery, not one number: median ratio "in conjunction with its confidence interval", quantiles and COD for level and variability, PRD and PRB for vertical equity, on holdout samples; "For each measure of AVM quality assurance, the analyst should consider a measure of reliability which explicitly considers such sampling error. Confidence intervals are the most commonly used measure of reliability." — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018), s.7.3.1-7.3.4](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf); [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018)](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- Outliers: "the mean and COV, and, to a lesser extent, the COD, are sensitive to extreme ratios"; holdout statistics "would be expected to differ" from in-sample ones and "If there are substantially worse quality assurance statistics in the hold out sample, recalibration of the AVM model may be necessary." — [IAAO Standard on AVMs (2018), s.7.3.6 and s.7.4](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)
- May 2026 draft principles: "Horizontal and vertical equity measures should be computed and examined"; "Conducting multiple tests may be required to ensure compliance"; "Extremely low variability should be examined to detect possible sales chasing or model overfitting"; "Ratio studies must employ measures of statistical significance - such as confidence intervals and hypothesis testing". — [IAAO exposure draft May 2026, s.8 and s.11](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)

#### European AVM Alliance
- "the Bias of a Statistical Valuation Method may be small even if few or no cases were actually valued accurately at all ... For this reason, Dispersion must also be assessed." — [EAA Standards 3rd ed., s.5.2](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)
- Accuracy "cannot be viewed independently from Coverage, due to the fact that by changing sample or considering only parts of it, Accuracy itself may appear higher or lower." — [EAA Standards 3rd ed., ch.5](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)
- Minimum reporting: an error histogram with bins no wider than 5%, and tables by property type, geographic area and price range showing at least the number of cases and the percentages within 10%, 15% and 20%. "Fair and objective like-for-like comparisons require the exact same measure of Dispersion when comparing the Accuracy of different Statistical Valuation Methods." — [EAA Standards 3rd ed., s.5.2 and s.5.5](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)
- "the degree to which the Confidence Level actually correlates with the Accuracy of the AVM result when compared with the Benchmark Value is key to the assessment of AVM Accuracy"; "Blind testing is critical to meaningful AVM Accuracy assessment." — [EAA Glossary](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Glossary_v23.pdf)

#### Testers, vendors and Freddie Mac on medians, tails and uncertainty
- Clear Capital: the median absolute error "hides the times the AVM was really far off; i.e. the outliers", and PPE10, though a standard measure, does not capture predictions that are very far from the benchmark. — [Clear Capital glossary](https://www.clearcapital.com/blog-avm-testing-glossary/)
- HouseCanary: "While MdPE and MdAPE are accurate descriptors of the center of the error distribution, other percentile statistics help determine the overall shape and variance of the errors"; it recommends MPE, MAPE, MdPE, MdAPE and PPE 5/10/20 together, and validation "for specific segments of the population as well as globally" (property type, geography). — [HouseCanary, Standards for Automated Property Valuation (2022)](https://www.housecanary.com/images/65b19f5d2af745c8a24e1f7c_AE-AVM_Standards_Whitepaper_min-1.pdf)
- HouseCanary's own page shows how much a headline hides: pre-listing MdAPE is 6.8% overall but 37% for sales between $50,000 and $100,000 and 15.4% for manufactured homes. — [HouseCanary accuracy page](https://www.housecanary.com/accuracy/)
- Freddie Mac: "When testing an AVM a lender should validate not only that the model is accurate and the differences from the sale price are small, but also that the FSD and confidence score are reliable", by checking that about 68% of sale prices fall within one reported FSD, about 80% within 1.28 and about 90% within 1.65. — [Freddie Mac, Metrics Matter](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheets/dougwhitepaper_metricsmatter.pdf)
- AVMetrics on confidence scores: "Calibration is distinct from accuracy"; to verify it "you need to observe performance across many thousands of valuations, segmented by score range", and calibration can hold nationally yet fail in individual counties. — [AVMetrics blog, 4 Apr 2026](https://www.avmetrics.net/2026/04/04/avm-confidence-score-calibration/)
- Francke (University of Amsterdam / Ortec Finance) frames accuracy as two layers: "Aggregate measures: Bias / dispersion / vertical inequality (IAAO Standard on ratio studies)" and "Individual measures: prediction intervals (or density)". — [Francke, IAAO presentation, 4 Dec 2024](https://iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/20241204-IAAO-Francke.pdf)
- Tree-based AVMs and uncertainty: for random forests and gradient boosted trees, "a main drawback of such methods is their limited ability to quantify prediction uncertainty"; plain conformal prediction gives confidence sets that "will be too large in certain geographical regions and too small in others", and spatially weighted calibration makes coverage more consistent (Oslo data; first posted 11 Dec 2023, revised 30 Jan 2025). — [Hjort, Hermansen, Pensar & Williams, arXiv:2312.06531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06531)
- A companion study on 29,993 Oslo transactions (2018-2019) finds conformalized quantile regression gives narrower confidence regions than split conformal inference on top of a random forest. — [Hjort (2022), PMLR 179:313-315](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v179/hjort22a.html)
- The loss a model is trained on should match the metric it is judged by: in Norway, training gradient boosted trees on a loss closer to the "share within +/-20%" measure improved that share (89.4% to 90.0%). — [Hjort, Pensar, Scheel & Sommervoll (2022)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:jpropr:v:39:y:2022:i:4:p:338-364)

### Inferences
- Is MdAPE the right headline? It is a legitimate and widely used headline: Zillow, Redfin, HouseCanary and ATTOM all lead with it, and the IAAO's 2026 draft calls it the statistic that expresses "the typical percentage error". It is not sufficient on its own, and lending-grade sources (AVMetrics, MISMO, EAA) prefer the share within 10% (and 20% in Europe) because it speaks to how often an estimate is usable.
- A defensible reporting set, assembled from the sources above: (1) MdAPE; (2) PPE10 and PPE20 (and PPE5 if comparing with portals); (3) signed bias, both mean and median percentage error; (4) a tail statistic or the full histogram in 5% bins (the EAA minimum), since medians and PPE both ignore how bad the misses are; (5) COD with median ratio, PRD and PRB for value-related bias; (6) coverage or hit rate, i.e. the share of sales the model could score; (7) calibration of the model's own uncertainty (does a stated plus/minus band contain the stated share of sale prices); (8) all of the above split by segment (resale versus off-plan, district, price band) with sample sizes and confidence intervals, on a blind, time-ordered holdout.
- The reader's figures already illustrate the single-metric problem: MdAPE 4.1% suggests a normal-equivalent sigma of about 6%, but 6% of estimates are more than 20% off, where a 6% normal sigma would predict well under 1%. The headline should therefore travel with PPE20 (or a 90th-percentile error) and the segment split (3.4% off-plan versus 6.0% resale).
- State the error formula explicitly (error divided by sale price is the EAA, portal and IAAO-ratio convention); as Steurer and Hill show, switching the denominator moves cases across the 10% line.
- PRD and PRB matter for a deal-finding use case in particular: a model that overvalues cheap units and undervalues expensive ones (regressive, PRD above 1.03 or negative PRB) would generate spurious "below market" flags concentrated at one end of the price range.
