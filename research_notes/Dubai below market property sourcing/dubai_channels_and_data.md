# Dubai: where distressed, auctioned and urgent-sale apartments surface, the data to find them, and buyer constraints (as of 15 November 2026)

Reading notes. Every source below was opened on 15 Nov 2026; the date after each source name is its publication or update date. Web pages were read through an automated page reader, so short quotes are as returned by it; three PDFs (Law 14 of 2008, Knight Frank Q1 2026, REIDIN H1 2026) were read as extracted text. English texts of Dubai laws are translations and the Arabic prevails. "Broker claim" marks weaker evidence. Anything I could not open is listed under Gaps as an unverified lead, never under Cited Findings. "Local file" means a file in this repository, not a web source.

## 1. Auctions: how real-estate auctions work in Dubai

### Takeaway
Court-ordered execution sales (mortgage foreclosures and other judgment debts) are the one genuinely forced-sale channel: the Dubai Courts execution judge orders the sale, the DLD (or another expert) values the property, bidding runs online on Emirates Auction, and a bidder needs a 20% deposit and the balance within about 10 days. Visible volume is very small (37 property lots on the platform on 15 Nov 2026, 17 of them in Dubai) and every documented outcome I found cleared at or above the reserve or valuation, because the opening threshold is the market valuation and it only steps down after failed sessions.

### Cited Findings

**Legal basis (Dubai Law No. 14 of 2008 Concerning Mortgages, issued 14 Aug 2008)**
- Art. 25: on default the mortgagee "must provide the debtor or person in possession of the mortgaged Property or Property Unit 30 days notice through the Notary Public before commencing execution proceedings." — [Law 14 of 2008, English translation hosted by visitdubai.com](https://www.visitdubai.com/-/media/Files/invest-in-dubai/corporate-legislation-docs/domestic-economic-legislation/en/court-mortgage-law-no-14-of-2008-of-the-emirate-of-dubai)
- Art. 26: if unpaid, "the execution judge shall, upon request of the Mortgagee/creditor order an attachment against the mortgaged Property so that it can be sold by public auction in accordance with the applicable procedures of the Department." — [Law 14 of 2008](https://www.visitdubai.com/-/media/Files/invest-in-dubai/corporate-legislation-docs/domestic-economic-legislation/en/court-mortgage-law-no-14-of-2008-of-the-emirate-of-dubai)
- Art. 27: the judge may postpone the auction "for up to 60 days and for one time only" if the debtor can repay in that period or the sale would cause the debtor substantial damage. Art. 28: otherwise the property is sold by public auction "within 30 days after the end of the relevant period". — [Law 14 of 2008](https://www.visitdubai.com/-/media/Files/invest-in-dubai/corporate-legislation-docs/domestic-economic-legislation/en/court-mortgage-law-no-14-of-2008-of-the-emirate-of-dubai)
- Art. 11: a contract clause giving the lender title on default, or allowing sale "without taking the statutory steps", is void (so no private repossession or bank-arranged private sale). Art. 30: if proceeds fall short the creditor "can claim the difference from the debtor". Art. 4: the mortgagee must be a bank or finance company licensed by the UAE Central Bank. Art. 24: off-plan units registered in the Interim Register can be mortgaged. Art. 31: the federal Civil Transactions and Civil Procedure codes apply to anything the law does not cover. — [Law 14 of 2008](https://www.visitdubai.com/-/media/Files/invest-in-dubai/corporate-legislation-docs/domestic-economic-legislation/en/court-mortgage-law-no-14-of-2008-of-the-emirate-of-dubai)
- A July 2026 legal column restates Arts. 25–28 the same way and adds that the new federal Civil Transactions Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025, Art. 1327) also requires notice to the debtor and court procedure before sale. — [Khaleej Times, Ashish Mehta, 12 Jul 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/dubai-mortgages-banks-repossess-homes-for-missed-loan-payments)

**Procedure, valuation and how the opening price moves (law-firm account)**
- After the 30-day notary notice, the lender applies to the Dubai Courts execution department (no separate judgment needed); the debtor is notified through the court and given 15 days to pay; the judge sets the date and "sessions are typically virtual, held through the Emirates Auctions portal." — [BSA Law, Hadiel Hussien, 18 Sep 2024](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/the-process-of-selling-a-mortgaged-property-through-a-court-supervised-auction-in-dubai/)
- The judge appoints an expert, "usually the Dubai Land Department", to assess market value; the mortgagee pays the cost and recovers it from proceeds. — [BSA Law, 18 Sep 2024](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/the-process-of-selling-a-mortgaged-property-through-a-court-supervised-auction-in-dubai/)
- First session: the highest bid must at least equal the valuation plus the mortgagee's expenses, otherwise the judge postpones. Later sessions: the price is reduced "by 5% each time until it reaches a maximum reduction of 25%". After that the auction is postponed three months and the property sells to the highest bidder provided the bid is not below 50% of the initial valuation. — [BSA Law, 18 Sep 2024](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/the-process-of-selling-a-mortgaged-property-through-a-court-supervised-auction-in-dubai/)
- Postponement can also be sought under Art. 288 of the federal Civil Procedure Law (five years of property revenue sufficient to settle the debt, or instalments within five years with guarantees). BSA notes foreclosure against residential property may need special approval in Dubai. — [BSA Law, 18 Sep 2024](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/the-process-of-selling-a-mortgaged-property-through-a-court-supervised-auction-in-dubai/)
- DLD's registration service for auction sales requires a "property valuation certificate" valid for six months "obtained specifically for the auction", an auction award letter, and an application letter from an official entity "such as Dubai Courts or Minors Affairs" where applicable. — [DLD e-service: Registration of a property sold by auction](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/)
- Older account (2012): only conventional banks appear able to use the judicial-sale route; Islamic financiers commonly use Ijara, where the institution already holds title and leases the unit. — [Hadef & Partners, Ashraf Sayed, 22 May 2012](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/inside-the-dubai-land-departments-property-auctions/)

**Emirates Auction, Dubai Courts and the DLD**
- Emirates Auction ran Dubai Courts' electronic sales of seized assets (cars and properties) in 2014, listing items on its website; assets are seized only after the judgment is final and the debtor has had 15 days to pay. — [The National, 17 Dec 2014](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/dubai-courts-earns-dh365m-from-auctioning-seized-assets-1.568273)
- In 2019 the DLD signed a cooperation agreement making Emirates Auction responsible for organising and managing the DLD's "selective real estate auctions for individuals, institutions, banks, and real estate developers", online or through auction houses; Emirates Auction cited 15 years of experience in judicial real-estate auctions. — [DLD / Emirates Auction press release via Zawya, 2019](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/dubai-land-department-inks-partnership-agreement-with-emirates-auction-nkhgmhtk)
- Emirates Auction's own FAQ refers to the court directly: a property can be viewed only if it is vacant and "the court has handed the key" to the company; the debtor, judges, prosecutors, court clerks, the debtor's lawyers and their relatives to the second degree may not bid; the sale is not final until title is issued, subject to the decisions of the judicial or government bodies; Emirates Auction "completes all procedures until the ownership of the property is issued". — [Emirates Auction FAQ (property)](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/9)
- DLD fees for an auction sale are paid "through the licensed auction company", i.e. the DLD treats auctioneers as licensed intermediaries. — [DLD e-service: auction registration](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/)
- Other auction venues named by a portal guide: DLD's eMart, Response Auction, Boli.ae, Galetti (commercial). — [Property Finder blog, 16 Nov 2025](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/real-estate-auction/). A private platform, Poly.AE, ran what Al Bayan called Dubai's first fully digital property auction (one City Walk two-bedroom flat, no reserve, opening bid AED 500,000; final price not disclosed). — [Al Bayan, c. May 2026 (Arabic)](https://www.albayan.ae/economy/business/real-estate/1270951)

**Who may bid**
- Ownership of real property in Dubai is restricted to UAE nationals, GCC nationals, companies wholly owned by them and public joint stock companies; non-UAE nationals may hold freehold, usufruct or leasehold (up to 99 years) only in areas the Ruler designates (Art. 4). Dispositions are invalid unless registered (Art. 9). — [Law No. 7 of 2006, Dubai Legislation Portal](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2006/Law%20No.%20%287%29%20of%202006.html)
- On Emirates Auction, "Freehold property is available in the auction" alongside property assigned to GCC citizens, and each lot states which it is; companies may bid through a company account; for GCC-designated property the company owner must be 100% Emirati and the company registered in Dubai. — [Emirates Auction FAQ](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/9)
- DLD's auction registration lists "Residency status: All" and accepts a valid passport from non-resident foreigners. — [DLD e-service: auction registration](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/)

**Deposit, payment deadline, fees**
- "The security deposit is 20% of the property price"; for property it must be a manager's cheque or bank transfer/deposit (credit cards are accepted for every auction type "except for Property Auctions"); it is fully refundable to losing bidders; one deposit per property bid on. — [Emirates Auction FAQ (deposits)](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/11)
- The balance plus fees is due "within maximum 10 days as of bid approval date"; failure "will result in the confiscation of the entire deposit amount"; the highest bidder cannot withdraw. — [Emirates Auction FAQ](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/9)
- BSA gives the same figures from the court side: deposit of at least 20% of the expert valuation, balance and expenses within 10 days of the session. — [BSA Law, 18 Sep 2024](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/the-process-of-selling-a-mortgaged-property-through-a-court-supervised-auction-in-dubai/)
- DLD fees on an auction sale. Purchaser: sale registration 2% of sale value, title deed AED 250, unit map AED 250, knowledge and innovation fees AED 10 each. Seller: sale registration 2%, plus auction supervision of 1% of sale value capped at AED 30,000 for public auctions or AED 10,000 for e-auctions. — [DLD e-service: auction registration](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/)
- The Emirates Auction listing page shows two platform rules: a bid in the last 3 minutes extends the lot by 3 minutes, and an auction that ended in the last 10 days can be reopened by a new bid at least 10% above the price plus admin fees. — [Emirates Auction properties page, read 15 Nov 2026](https://www.emiratesauction.com/properties)
- Dated (2012, physical DLD auctions): 20% of reserve by cheque, balance in 7–10 days "extendable with DLD consent" where the buyer seeks mortgage finance, a registration fee then quoted as "1% of the bid price plus AED 315" (superseded by the 2% on the current DLD page), and DLD cancels the bid and returns the cheque if incurable defects are found. — [Hadef & Partners, 22 May 2012](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/inside-the-dubai-land-departments-property-auctions/)
- Conflict: a portal guide covering several platforms says the deposit is "usually 5–10% of the property's expected value" — [Property Finder blog, 16 Nov 2025](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/real-estate-auction/); contradicted for court sales by the 20% in [Emirates Auction FAQ](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/11) and [BSA Law](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/the-process-of-selling-a-mortgaged-property-through-a-court-supervised-auction-in-dubai/)

**Volumes**
- Snapshot on 15 Nov 2026: 37 property lots listed on Emirates Auction ("Showing 1-37"): Dubai 17, Ajman 11, Sharjah 9; labels "Freehold" and "GCC"; apartments, studios, villas, land, commercial buildings and "part of property" lots; listed amounts from about AED 159,116 to AED 56,243,056; seller type (court, bank, developer) not shown. — [Emirates Auction properties page, read 15 Nov 2026](https://www.emiratesauction.com/properties)
- Dubai Courts' 2025 annual report, as reported by Al Bayan: "smart auctions" raised more than AED 1.924 billion in 2025 (all asset classes; no property breakdown); 64,329 of 86,315 execution files closed. — [Al Bayan, c. Aug 2026 (Arabic)](https://www.albayan.ae/news/uae/dubai/1544062)
- Jan–Nov 2014: Dubai Courts e-auctions of seized assets raised Dh365,575,450 against pre-auction valuations of Dh283 million. — [The National, 17 Dec 2014](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/dubai-courts-earns-dh365m-from-auctioning-seized-assets-1.568273)
- 2011–2012: first foreclosure sale under the 2008 law was a Springs villa at Dh1.22m in May 2011; in Dec 2011 three auctions offered 19 properties and eight foreclosed homes sold; the DLD expected "up to 80" foreclosed properties to be auctioned in 2012. — [The National, 7 Dec 2011](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/dubai-s-first-bulk-auction-of-repossessed-properties-1.450820); [Hadef & Partners, 22 May 2012](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/inside-the-dubai-land-departments-property-auctions/)
- 2010: one Al Tamimi partner oversaw up to 70 foreclosure cases from 12 banks with 10 judgments obtained; "hundreds" of buyers had received DLD cancellation notices on behalf of developers. — [The National, 23 Jun 2010](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/foreclosure-auctions-are-likely-to-hit-home-1.552811)
- For scale: Dubai recorded 3,602 ready and 7,042 off-plan residential deals in October 2026. — [Khaleej Times citing ValuStrat, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-residential-values-post-first-monthly-gain-since-february)

**Sale prices relative to reserve, valuation or market**
- Dec 2011: all eight foreclosed properties "sold above their reserve prices"; one villa listed at Dh3.5m sold for Dh5.6m; Cluttons said earlier reserves had been "just too high". — [The National, 7 Dec 2011](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/dubai-s-first-bulk-auction-of-repossessed-properties-1.450820)
- May 2012: four of five repossessed Springs villas sold, at about 15–30% above the asking (opening) price, with more than 160 people attending. — [The National, 1 May 2012](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/dubai-villas-sold-at-auction-as-buyers-return-1.395799)
- 2012: at auctions Hadef attended most lots sold, with "bidders paying 5% to 40% higher than the reserve price". — [Hadef & Partners, 22 May 2012](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/inside-the-dubai-land-departments-property-auctions/)
- 2014: Dubai Courts' auction sale prices "exceeded valuations by over Dh82 million" in aggregate (cars and property together). — [The National, 17 Dec 2014](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/dubai-courts-earns-dh365m-from-auctioning-seized-assets-1.568273)
- Al Bayan's account of the 2025 court report argues open online bidding pushes final prices closer to market value. — [Al Bayan, c. Aug 2026](https://www.albayan.ae/news/uae/dubai/1544062)
- Weak evidence (marketing guide citing other guides): court and repossession purchases "historically closed about 10–30% below open-market value", and in 2026 vacant lots in good buildings reportedly bid "to within a few percent" of market. — [Real Estate Club Dubai, 15 Jun 2026](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/investment/dubai-property-auctions-2026-emirates-auction-bank-repossessions-guide)

### Inferences
- The price mechanics cap the discount: a lot cannot sell below valuation plus costs in the first session, then only 5% lower per failed session to a 25% floor, and only after a further three months as low as 50% of valuation. A discount to the court valuation therefore requires that nobody bid at the earlier, higher thresholds, which points to lots with a problem (occupied, undivided shares, defects, GCC-only title) rather than ordinary apartments.
- The reference is a DLD valuation up to six months old. In a falling market (ValuStrat −10% between February and August 2026, see section 6) a stale valuation can sit above market, so "sold at valuation" can still be overpaying, and "25% below valuation" is less than 25% below market.
- 17 Dubai lots listed against roughly 10,600 residential deals in October 2026 is under 0.2% of monthly turnover. Auctions are a niche source, not a pipeline.
- The 20% manager's-cheque deposit and 10-day balance make this effectively a cash channel; Bayut reports mortgage approvals taking up to 8 weeks (section 7).
- The 10%-over-bid window means a winning bid is not secure for 10 days, during which the deposit stays committed.
- Fee arithmetic favours the auction buyer slightly: 2% DLD fee instead of the 4% normally borne by a resale buyer and no broker commission, about 4 points of price, provided the conditions of sale do not pass the seller's 2% or arrears to the buyer.

### Gaps
- No official count of properties offered and sold per year at Dubai Courts / Emirates Auction, and no property-only split of the AED 1.924bn for 2025. Emirates Auction says results are under "The Previous Auction" on its site; I did not browse lot-level results.
- Auction frequency is not published in any page I opened (lots carry individual end dates; the site has an "Auction Calendar").
- Emirates Auction's own fee or buyer's premium for property is not stated in its FAQ ("fees" are mentioned without a schedule).
- Federal Civil Procedure Law article numbers for the 20% deposit, 10-day payment, 5%/25%/50% reductions and the 10% over-bid were not confirmed against the statute text; they rest on BSA Law and the Emirates Auction pages.
- No transaction-based study of Dubai auction prices versus market value exists in what I found; evidence is the 2011–2014 reports above plus unsourced guides.
- Unverified leads (not opened): a DLD–Dubai Courts–Digital Dubai memorandum to build a centralised auction-information platform and a licensing mechanism for auction companies (economymiddleeast.com returned HTTP 403); DLD news items on eMart (launched 4 Nov 2013; "33 properties sold for AED 284m in ten months") and on licensing a second firm, "Online Auction", in July 2019 (dubailand.gov.ae news URLs returned 404); a Dubizzle eMart guide (HTTP 410); Al Tamimi's mortgage-enforcement articles (HTTP 307). Whether eMart still operates is unconfirmed.

## 2. Off-plan defaults: Article 11 of Dubai Law No. 13 of 2008 as amended

### Takeaway
Article 11 lets a developer act against a defaulting off-plan buyer without a court, but only through the DLD: a 30-day DLD notice, then remedies that depend on construction progress, namely keep the contract and claim the balance, ask the DLD to auction the unit (only above 80% completion), or terminate and keep up to 40% of the contract value (25% below 60% completion). Terminated units go back to the developer for resale, so they mostly reach the market as ordinary developer inventory; the stock a buyer can actually reach is the pre-default resale by the squeezed purchaser ("below original price" listings).

### Cited Findings
- Law No. 19 of 2017 (issued and effective 18 Oct 2017) replaced Article 11: the developer notifies the DLD on the prescribed form; the DLD serves the purchaser a written 30-day notice and may mediate; if still in default the DLD issues a document confirming the developer's compliance and the project's completion percentage. — [Law No. 19 of 2017, Dubai Legislation Portal](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2017/Law%20No.%20%2819%29%20of%202017%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2813%29%20of%202008%20Regulating%20the%20Interim%20Real%20Property%20Register%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)
- Law No. 19 of 2020 (issued 24 Nov 2020, effective on Gazette publication) replaced Article 11 again and is the current text I found. Remedies "without courts or arbitration": above 80% complete, (1) keep the agreement, retain payments and claim the balance, or (2) ask the DLD to sell the unit by public auction to recover the amounts owed, the purchaser bearing the sale costs, or (3) terminate and retain up to 40% of "the value of the Real Property Unit stipulated in the Off-plan Sale agreement"; 60–80% complete, terminate and retain up to 40%; below 60% with work started, terminate and retain up to 25%. — [Law No. 19 of 2020, Dubai Legislation Portal](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2020/Law%20No.%20%2819%29%20of%202020%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2813%29%20of%202008%20Regulating%20the%20Interim%20Real%20Property%20Register%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)
- Refund timing after termination: the excess is repaid "within one (1) year from the termination" or "within sixty (60) days from the date of resale of the Real Property Unit to another purchaser", whichever is earlier. — [Law No. 19 of 2020](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2020/Law%20No.%20%2819%29%20of%202020%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2813%29%20of%202008%20Regulating%20the%20Interim%20Real%20Property%20Register%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)
- The 2020 text applies to agreements made before or after it, preserves earlier measures, is a matter of public order (non-compliance voids the act), excludes pure land sales, and keeps the purchaser's right to go to court or arbitration. — [Law No. 19 of 2020](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2020/Law%20No.%20%2819%29%20of%202020%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2813%29%20of%202008%20Regulating%20the%20Interim%20Real%20Property%20Register%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)
- Conflict between sources on the "work not started" case. The 2017 text let the developer terminate and keep up to 30% of amounts paid, refunding the rest within 60 days — [Law No. 19 of 2017](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2017/Law%20No.%20%2819%29%20of%202017%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2813%29%20of%202008%20Regulating%20the%20Interim%20Real%20Property%20Register%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html); the 2020 text, paragraph (b), requires a full refund where work has not started for reasons beyond the developer's control or RERA cancels the project — [Law No. 19 of 2020](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2020/Law%20No.%20%2819%29%20of%202020%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2813%29%20of%202008%20Regulating%20the%20Interim%20Real%20Property%20Register%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html); a current press explainer still cites the 2017 amendment and the 30% rule — [Khaleej Times, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/missed-off-plan-payments-dubai-developer-legally-do)
- The same explainer confirms the rest of the scheme as applied in 2026: DLD notice "giving the buyer 30 days to settle or reach an amicable resolution", a DLD certificate of completion percentage, deduction of "no more than 40 per cent of the unit's value" above 60%, 25% below 60%, public auction as an option above 80%. — [Khaleej Times, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/missed-off-plan-payments-dubai-developer-legally-do)
- A law-firm summary describes the auction option as the developer asking the Land Department to sell at public auction, the buyer bearing the sale expenses. — [Dr Hassan Elhais via Legal 500, 4 Jun 2024](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-arab-emirates/real-estate-and-construction/what-happens-if-a-buyer-defaults-on-payments-after-buying-off-plan-property-in-dubai)
- The DLD–Emirates Auction agreement expressly covers auctions for "real estate developers", and the DLD registers auction sales through licensed auction companies. — [Zawya press release, 2019](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/dubai-land-department-inks-partnership-agreement-with-emirates-auction-nkhgmhtk); [DLD e-service: auction registration](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/)
- 2010 precedent for where cancelled units go: "hundreds" of buyers received DLD cancellation notices; under the then Executive Resolution a developer past 60% completion could deduct 40% of the price, terminate and rent the unit out; developers were considering keeping repossessed units as rentals because they feared low auction bids. — [The National, 23 Jun 2010](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/foreclosure-auctions-are-likely-to-hit-home-1.552811)
- 2026 scale indicator: Emaar's unit cancellations rose from an average of 700 to 1,000 during the regional conflict and fell to 550 after the ceasefire (the period these counts cover is not stated); Emaar was collecting close to 96% of payments that had initially been delayed. — [Khaleej Times, Mohamed Alabbar at AIM Congress, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-property-prices-could-adjust-5-10-as-new-supply-comes-by-2027-says-alabbar)
- Developers are not discounting cancelled or unsold stock much: 44 of 717 off-plan projects launched since July 2023 (about 6%) cut prices by 5% or more after end-February 2026, 28 (4%) were selling below launch price, first cuts recorded in June 2026. — [Khaleej Times citing fäm Properties, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-developers-hold-off-plan-pricing-as-only-6-projects-cut-prices-by-5-or-more-since-february)
- Broker claim: secondary off-plan apartments "in many cases trade 10–15% below original values" and "buyers that are looking for distressed sales can find some good deals in that space" (Mario Volpi, Eva Real Estate). — [Fortune, 1 Jun 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/property-prices-down-dubai/)
- Not Dubai: the 2025 law that lets developers rescind after a notarised notice and a 60-day cure period is Abu Dhabi Law No. 2 of 2025. — [The Law Reporters, 20 Aug 2025](https://thelawreporters.com/uae-developers-can-now-terminate-off-plan-spas-without-court-action)

### Inferences
- The refund clock runs from "resale of the Real Property Unit to another purchaser", so the statute itself assumes the developer resells terminated units. Combined with the fäm data, such units re-enter at or near list price, not as bargains.
- The retention cap creates the motivated seller. A purchaser who has paid up to 40% of the price on a building past 60% completion loses all of it on termination, so selling the contract at a loss up to the amount already paid is rational. That is the economic source of "below OP" listings and it is far larger than the auction channel: off-plan was 72% of Dubai transactions in Q1 2026 (section 6).
- These resales are assignments of off-plan contracts (Oqood), which need developer consent and carry the remaining payment plan; the reader's model scores registered sales and would see them only once registered.

### Gaps
- No public statistics on Article 11 notices, terminations or DLD-requested auctions, and no evidence of how many Article 11 auctions are actually held or whether they appear on Emirates Auction.
- No data on the prices at which developers resell terminated units.
- Whether any amendment to Article 11 followed Law 19 of 2020 was not established; I found none. The contradiction between the 2020 text and the October 2026 press explainer should be resolved against the Arabic text before the 30% rule is quoted.
- Unverified leads (not opened): Al Tamimi "Developers in Dubai can terminate without court order" (HTTP 307); HFW briefing of February 2018.

## 3. Listings: "distress", "below original price" and price-reduced listings on the portals

### Takeaway
"Distress", "urgent sale" and "below OP" are agent-written labels that no portal verifies, and "below OP" compares the asking price with what the seller paid, not with market value. All three portals forbid scraping and compiling listing databases, and Property Finder's API is limited to agencies and approved partners, so listings must be read manually or licensed. Price-reduction statistics come from a third-party tracker and press reports; I found no official or portal-published days-on-market series.

### Cited Findings

**Terms of use**

### Inferences
- From the Fortune figures, 3,292 of about 27,000 tracked listings is roughly 12% with a price cut by end-May 2026 (my arithmetic). Cuts are from the seller's own first asking price, so they show motivation, not a discount to market.
- "Below OP" is informative only relative to when the seller bought. Prices rose about 60% from 2022 to early 2025 (section 6), so a 2022 buyer selling "below OP" is deeply below market, while a 2025 launch priced above its area (fäm: the median project launched since 2025 was priced 4.2% above its area median) can be "below OP" and still at or above market.
- Because the reader has a valuation model, the label can be tested instead of trusted: score the asking price for the stated unit and rank by model spread. Given the terms above, inputs have to be entered by hand, obtained from a broker, or licensed.
- If genuine distress trades through broker networks before portals, as the broker sources say, portal "distress" pages are adversely selected toward listings that did not sell privately.

## 4. Official and commercial data

### Takeaway

## 5. Size of discounts reported for distressed or auctioned Dubai property

### Takeaway
Measured evidence of realised distress discounts in Dubai is thin. The documented auction results (2011–2012, 2014) cleared above reserve or valuation, and the familiar "10–30% below market" range comes from brokers and marketing guides. For 2026 the firm numbers are market-wide (index down about 10% from February to August), while the distress-specific figures (luxury closing 20–25% below pre-conflict valuations, off-plan resales 10–15% below original price, pockets down 15–20%) are agent statements measured against pre-conflict or purchase prices, not against current market value.

### Cited Findings

**2008–2012**
- By mid-2010 prices had fallen about 50% from peak, with Bank of America Merrill Lynch expecting a further 15%; a lawyer expected foreclosure auctions to produce "highly discounted prices" (an expectation, not a result). — [The National, 23 Jun 2010](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/foreclosure-auctions-are-likely-to-hit-home-1.552811)
- By 2012 prices had fallen "by as much as 60 per cent since 2009"; Fortune's 2026 retrospective puts the 2009 crash at 40–50%. — [The National, 1 May 2012](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/dubai-villas-sold-at-auction-as-buyers-return-1.395799); [Fortune, 1 Jun 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/property-prices-down-dubai/)
- Actual foreclosure auctions in that trough sold above reserve: all eight lots in Dec 2011; 15–30% above the asking price in May 2012; 5–40% above reserve at auctions Hadef attended. — [The National, 7 Dec 2011](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/dubai-s-first-bulk-auction-of-repossessed-properties-1.450820); [The National, 1 May 2012](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/dubai-villas-sold-at-auction-as-buyers-return-1.395799); [Hadef & Partners, 22 May 2012](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/inside-the-dubai-land-departments-property-auctions/)

### Inferences
- The 2026 agent figures use the wrong base for the reader's purpose. A sale "20–25% below pre-conflict valuation" in a market that had itself fallen about 10% is roughly 10–15% below contemporaneous market value, and less in segments that fell more than the average. Likewise "10–15% below original price" says nothing about market value without the purchase date.
- Local auction history argues against expecting an auction discount as such: reserves start at valuation and competitive bidding has pushed results above it. Where a discount exists it is compensation for cash, speed and title or occupancy risk.
- The project's own validation is consistent with these magnitudes: registry-proven same-unit pairs show a realised buyer's edge of about +14% on flagged below-fair-value purchases, and spreads beyond −35% are treated as probable data noise (local file `/home/user/Dubai_real_state_dashboard/reports/distress_validation_report.md`, lines 113–120).

### Gaps
- No transaction-based estimate of a forced-sale or distress discount for Dubai was found from any consultancy (Knight Frank, CBRE, JLL, Cavendish Maxwell, ValuStrat, Property Monitor) or in the press. Reuters and Bloomberg coverage was not reachable; Cavendish Maxwell and AGBI pages returned HTTP 403.
- Unverified leads (not opened, Zawya returned HTTP 400): a 2020 report that owners were "listing residential properties at a 10 to 15 percent discount on the current market price" during COVID-19, mainly villas and townhouses; an April 2011 report that speculators in 2009–early 2010 sold at "even a 50 per cent discount".

## 6. Market context 2025–2026 and what it implies for distressed supply

### Takeaway
Dubai's five-and-a-half-year rise ended in February 2026: a regional war that began on 28 February cut the ValuStrat index by about 10% by August, with the first monthly rise (+0.1%) in October and values 5.7% below a year earlier. Volumes fell far more than prices, sellers have mostly held, and forced selling so far is "selective". The conditions that create distress are still building rather than cleared: AED 13.5bn of deferred bank loans began to be reclassified in September 2026 with a 6–12 month lag expected, and S&P expects 2027–2028 deliveries to press hardest on investor-driven apartment segments.

### Cited Findings

**Before the war**
- Fitch (May 2025): a "moderate correction" from H2 2025 through 2026 not expected to exceed 15%; deliveries of about 30,000 units in 2024, 90,000 in 2025 and 120,000 planned for 2026; prices up about 60% from 2022 to Q1 2025; banks' property exposure 14% of gross loans at end-2024. — [The National, 29 May 2025](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/2025/05/29/dubai-property-prices-expected-to-fall-by-15-on-supply-glut-this-year-fitch-says)
- UBS (Sept 2025): Dubai's bubble risk "has surged for a second consecutive year and reached an elevated level"; real prices about 50% above five years earlier; building permits suggest construction could reach 2017 levels. — [UBS media release, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.ubs.com/global/de/media/display-page-ndp/en-20250923-grebi25.html)
- Moody's (Feb 2026): the market to cool gradually over 12–18 months; banks' real-estate and construction lending 12% of loans (19% in Dec 2021); NPL ratio a record-low 2.9% at June 2025; "most property transactions are still paid in cash". — [Khaleej Times, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-banks-resilient-despite-real-estate-growth-moodys-ratings)

### Inferences
- Distress so far is contained by three buffers visible in the sources: sellers able to hold (large gains since 2020–2022 and mostly cash purchases), bank deferrals, and developer forbearance (Emaar recovering 96% of delayed payments). Two of the three are temporary. On the EnterpriseAM timeline, reclassification of deferred loans from September 2026 plus a 6–12 month lag puts any rise in mortgage enforcement in roughly March–September 2027, and Law 14's notice, valuation and auction steps add months more.
- Off-plan payment-plan stress should arrive before mortgage foreclosures, because off-plan is about 70% of transactions and handovers trigger the largest instalments. The likely locations are the high-pipeline apartment districts Knight Frank lists (Jumeirah Village Circle, Business Bay, Dubailand Residence Complex), matching S&P's "investor-driven apartment segments".
- Price indices disagree on the size of the fall because they measure different things: ValuStrat's valuation-based index shows −10% from February to August, while transaction averages (Knight Frank/REIDIN psf) were still rising into Q1 and are shaped by which units trade. The same split shows against the last cycle: Knight Frank puts apartments 25% above the 2014 peak (Q1 2026, average psf including new stock) while ValuStrat puts older freehold apartments 9% below it (August 2026). A model trained on registered sales inherits that composition effect and lags a fast move.
- October's uptick in ready sales with flat prices means buyers looking for distress are competing with returning end-users; the window of weak competition was March–August 2026.

## 7. Buyer frictions and costs, and how they reduce the net discount

### Takeaway
An ordinary resale costs the buyer about 6–7% of the price before any finance costs (4% DLD fee, about 2.1% agent commission, trustee and small fees); Bayut puts the all-in at 7–10%. A headline discount below that range is consumed by costs, and a 10–15% discount nets to mid-single digits. Distressed purchases add their own costs: cash and speed, clearing the seller's mortgage before transfer, service-charge arrears that block the transfer, and sitting tenants who cannot be removed without 12 months' notarised notice.

### Cited Findings

**Transaction costs**
- DLD's fee list for a mortgaged-property sale: sale fee 4% of the sale value; registration trustee ("registrar sale") fee AED 2,100 below AED 500,000 and AED 4,200 at AED 500,000 or more; title deed AED 250; unit map AED 250; knowledge and innovation fees AED 10 each; mortgage registration 0.25% of the mortgage value; mortgage release AED 1,290 plus AED 315 registrar fee. — [DLD e-service: Registering the sale of a mortgaged property](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/registering-the-sale-of-a-mortgaged-property/)
- Portal cost guide: DLD transfer fee 4% "usually" paid by the buyer; trustee fee AED 2,000 or AED 4,000 plus 5% VAT; agent commission about 2% plus 5% VAT; developer NOC about AED 500–5,000; mortgage registration 0.25% of the loan plus about AED 290; bank arrangement fee 0.5–1% of the loan plus VAT; valuation AED 2,500–3,500 plus VAT; total buyer fees estimated at 7–10% of the price. — [MyBayut, updated 20 Oct 2026](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/cost-buying-property-dubai/)
- At auction the purchaser's DLD registration fee is 2% (seller 2% plus the supervision fee). — [DLD e-service: auction registration](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/)

**Settling the seller's mortgage**
- A mortgaged unit cannot be sold without the lender's approval (Art. 10). — [Law 14 of 2008](https://www.visitdubai.com/-/media/Files/invest-in-dubai/corporate-legislation-docs/domestic-economic-legislation/en/court-mortgage-law-no-14-of-2008-of-the-emirate-of-dubai)
- The DLD procedure needs a liability letter from the bank and three manager's cheques (to the bank for the debt, to the seller for the remainder, to the DLD for the 4%); the sale is completed only after the bank issues the mortgage release letter. — [DLD e-service: mortgaged property sale](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/registering-the-sale-of-a-mortgaged-property/)

**Cash, speed and financing**
- Auction: 20% deposit by manager's cheque or transfer, balance and fees within a maximum of 10 days of bid approval, deposit confiscated otherwise; viewing only if the unit is vacant and the court has handed over the key. — [Emirates Auction FAQ (deposits)](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/11); [Emirates Auction FAQ (property)](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/9)
- Private distress sales: mortgage approvals "can take up to 8 weeks" while cash buyers close in a couple of weeks or less, and urgent sellers favour cash; a mortgage is based on the lower of price or valuation (Bayut). Distressed deals are "generally cash" and close in 7 to 14 days (Gulf News, 2019). — [MyBayut, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/distressed-properties-dubai/); [Gulf News, 15 May 2019](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/buying-distressed-properties-in-uae-what-you-need-to-know-1.63916830)

**Service-charge arrears**
- Law No. 6 of 2019, Art. 32: the management entity has a lien on every unit for unpaid service charges and a unit "may not be disposed of unless these charges are paid"; after a 30-day RERA-approved notice the claim is enforceable by the execution judge at the Rental Disputes Centre, who may order the unit sold by public auction. Art. 16(b): the owner stays liable even if a tenant was meant to pay. The law has no express provision making a purchaser liable for a previous owner's arrears. — [Law No. 6 of 2019, Dubai Legislation Portal (issued 4 Sep 2019)](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2019/Law%20No.%20%286%29%20of%202019%20Concerning%20Ownership%20of%20Jointly%20Owned%20Real%20Property%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)
- The DLD FAQ confirms that a property can be "blocked for unpaid service charges" and that the landlord pays service fees. — [DLD FAQ](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/frequently-asked-questions/)

**Sitting tenants (Law No. 26 of 2007 as amended by Law No. 33 of 2008)**
- Art. 25(2): on expiry of the lease the landlord may seek eviction only for demolition or reconstruction, major restoration, personal use by the owner or a first-degree relative (who owns no suitable alternative), or because the owner "wishes to sell"; the tenant must be notified of the reasons at least 12 months before the eviction date through a Notary Public or by registered mail. Art. 26: after a personal-use eviction the unit cannot be re-let for two years (residential). Arts. 13–14: changes to lease terms, including rent, need 90 days' notice before expiry. — [Law No. 33 of 2008, Dubai Legislation Portal (issued 1 Dec 2008)](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2009/Law%20No.%20%2833%29%20of%202008%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2826%29%20of%202007.html)
- Art. 28 of Law 26 of 2007: "The transfer of ownership of the leased property to a new owner shall not affect the tenant's right" to stay under the lease; a buyer takes the unit subject to the tenant's rights and cannot end the tenancy merely because it bought the property. — [Khaleej Times, Ashish Mehta, 20 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/can-landlord-dubai-evict-sell-property-uae-law)
- Since late summer 2023 the Rental Disputes Centre has accepted in "at least 10 cases" that a 12-month notice served by the seller passes to the buyer (previously the buyer had to serve a new notice); RDC judgments "are not always used as precedent"; "you cannot evict someone in order to re-let it". — [Dr Hassan Elhais via Legal 500, 14 Feb 2024](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-arab-emirates/real-estate-and-construction/navigating-uncertainty-new-shift-in-dubai%27s-eviction-policies-raises-tenant-concerns)
- DLD FAQ: a new owner who keeps the tenants becomes their landlord, may not raise the security deposit, and can ask tenants to leave only if the owner or a relative wants to live in the property. — [DLD FAQ](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/frequently-asked-questions/)

**Ownership limits**
- Non-GCC buyers can acquire only in designated areas; auction lots are labelled Freehold or GCC. — [Law No. 7 of 2006](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2006/Law%20No.%20%287%29%20of%202006.html); [Emirates Auction FAQ](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/9)

### Inferences
- Worked example (my arithmetic from the fees above) for a cash resale at AED 1,500,000: DLD 4% AED 60,000, commission 2.1% AED 31,500, trustee AED 4,200, title deed, map and small fees about AED 520, total about AED 96,200 or 6.4%, before any NOC cost. Reselling later costs the next seller a commission as well, so a buyer who intends to realise the gain by resale needs a gross discount of roughly 8–9% just to break even.
- Net of costs, the documented 2026 discount ranges shrink sharply: 10% gross is about 3–4% net, 15% is about 8–9% net. The project's measured edge of about +14% on flagged purchases is of the same order as the round-trip cost plus a modest margin.
- The sellers most likely to be distressed are the leveraged ones, and those sales are the slowest and most cash-intensive for the buyer: the buyer's funds clear the seller's bank debt before title moves, and the sale waits on the bank's release letter.
- Because arrears block disposal rather than transfer to the buyer by statute, they are in practice negotiated out of the price or paid at completion; at a court auction the conditions of sale decide, so the service-charge statement must be checked before bidding.
- A tenanted flat is worth less to an owner-occupier for a legal reason, not because the seller is distressed: vacant possession needs lease expiry plus 12 months' notarised notice, and an investor cannot evict to re-let. A model that does not see occupancy will read that as a below-market price.
- A cash buyer who can close in days holds the scarce resource in this market. Every source that describes genuine distress deals describes cash, speed and tolerance of as-is condition as the price of the discount.

### Gaps
- DLD's fee pages do not state who pays each fee on a standard resale; the 2%/2% split is explicit only for auction sales, and a statement that the 4% is shared equally unless agreed otherwise (Executive Council Resolution No. 30 of 2013) was seen only in a search snippet.
- Typical size of service-charge arrears on distressed units, and how Emirates Auction conditions of sale allocate them, were not found.
- Central Bank mortgage rules (loan-to-value caps, cash down-payment minimums, arrears timelines) were not opened, so financing limits for non-resident and resident buyers are not covered here.
- Time actually taken from default to auction in recent cases is unknown; the only timing evidence is the statute and a 2011 case where the sale came more than a year after the court order (The National, 7 Dec 2011).
