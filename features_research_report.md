# Research Report: Price Drivers for a Dubai Apartment Fair-Value Model

*Prepared 2026-07-04. Baseline: HistGradientBoosting on log(AED/sqft), 24 months of DLD closed transactions (~320k rows), CV MedAPE ≈ 5.9%.*

## (a) Executive summary

Three independent evidence streams — hedonic literature, Kaggle competition post-mortems, and industry AVM disclosures — converge on the same conclusion: **the biggest untapped error reductions for a transaction-level model come from (1) unit- and building-level physical attributes (floor, age, view proxies), (2) transaction-history features engineered from data we already hold (prior sale of the same unit, spatial/temporal comp statistics, target encodings), and (3) income-side anchors (rents, listings)** — not from macro variables.

Key evidence points:

- **Location dominates, but we encode it weakly.** Zillow reports location (lat/lon, ZIP, school district) drives the largest share of predicted value in the Neural Zestimate; our location signal is categorical (district, project, nearest-metro *name*). Hedonic studies quantify what the categoricals hide: CBRE finds Dubai properties within 500 m of a metro station price **18–25% above** comparable units 1 km away; Hong Kong high-rise studies find **~11%/~22%** premiums for partial/full views; floor-level premiums are positive but diminishing with height. These are within-project effects our model literally cannot see today — and at a 5.9% MedAPE, an invisible 10–25% within-building spread is plausibly the dominant residual source.
- **Industry AVM error gaps are explained by data, not algorithms.** Zestimate: **2.4% median error on-market vs ~7.5% off-market**; HouseCanary: **2.7% postlist vs 7.5% prelist**. The ~5pp difference is the listing price + market-reaction signal. Our 5.9% on closed DLD transactions is already near "off-market AVM grade"; the roadmap item (listing prices) is the single largest known lever. AVMs also universally blend a **repeat-sales component** — the prior transaction price of the same unit — which we lack.
- **Kaggle's transferable lessons are feature-engineering patterns, not models.** Ames top solutions: neighborhood was the strongest predictor and *neighborhood × quality interactions* plus log-target beat model complexity. Sberbank's 1st place: per-segment models + residual analysis; crucially, **macro features failed** — the winner corrected the market trend with hand-tuned "magic number" scaling constants because no macro variable predicted the regime change at the train/test boundary. NVIDIA's Grandmasters Playbook and Kaggle folklore consistently emphasize out-of-fold **target encoding**, **group statistics** (median/std/count per group×period), train-test shift EDA, and strict temporal validation.
- **Dubai macro drivers (oil, EIBOR, USD, golden visa, supply pipeline) shape the market-level trend, not the cross-section.** Our model already carries a time trend; macro joins mainly help trend extrapolation and cold-start areas. The Sberbank experience is a direct warning that they can also *hurt* under regime shifts. Two exceptions with genuine cross-sectional content: **area-level supply pipeline** (2026 delivery pressure is concentrated in JVC/Business Bay/Dubai South non-prime stock, forecast 5–10% corrections there) and **golden-visa threshold effects** (demand concentration at/just above AED 2M).
- **Dubai open data can supply most of it.** Dubai Pulse hosts `dld_units-open` (freehold unit attributes), `dld_buildings-open`, `dld_projects-open` (developer, completion status/dates), and `dld_rent_contracts-open` (Ejari); RERA's Mollak service-charge index is per-project AED/sqft; OSM gives coordinates for metro/coastline/landmarks; CBUAE publishes daily EIBOR; DLD publishes the monthly RPPI.

## (c) Do-first shortlist

1. **Target encodings + group statistics from data we already have (#3, #12, #13, #14, #16).** Zero new data, days not weeks, and the Kaggle evidence says this class is where most tabular wins come from. Out-of-fold smoothed project/building encodings, rolling counts, area momentum lags, within-project size percentile, golden-visa threshold flag. Realistic expectation: several tenths of a pp MedAPE, which by the optimizer's ≥0.05pp acceptance bar is a large win.
2. **Repeat-sale prior-price feature (#1).** Extend the snapshot's history (the open transactions file goes back to 2004; keep 24m for training rows, longer for feature lookback), fuzzy-match same-unit resales. This is the single feature that lets the model see unobserved unit quality. Dubai's high turnover makes coverage meaningful.
3. **Units + buildings + projects join (#2, #7, #10, #21).** Floor, building age, floors count, parking count, developer. This is the Phase-2 "buildings/units metadata" item — the hedonic literature says floor and age are the two largest missing physical attributes; do it before listings because it's free open data.
4. **Geocode buildings once, then harvest continuous geospatial features (#4, #8, #9).** One geocoding investment (OSM/Makani lookup by building/project name) unlocks continuous metro distance, coast/Burj distance, H3 cells, and k-NN spatial comps — replacing three categorical location proxies with the representation every top AVM uses.
5. **Ejari building-level rental yield (#5).** Highest-value external join after metadata: an income-approach anchor orthogonal to sales comps, from an open Dubai Pulse dataset with the same project/building keys.

Defer: listing prices (#6) are the biggest single lever but the highest cost/fragility (scraping, entity matching, ToS); macro joins (#18–20) last, and only as trend/cold-start aids with time-sliced validation.

## (d) Validation cautions

- **Target/group encodings must be fold-fitted.** Compute encodings out-of-fold inside each `TimeSeriesSplit` training window (never on the full 24 months) and add smoothing toward the district prior for thin projects. This is "the easiest way to fake performance through leakage" in Kaggle experience.
- **All rolling/comp/count/momentum features need strict as-of joins.** Every trailing statistic (incl. the existing 60-day project median, and new k-NN comps, counts, momentum, Ejari yields) must use only transactions/contracts dated strictly before the row's `INSTANCE_DATE`, with a small embargo gap if registration dates lag deal dates.
- **Repeat-sales feature: use only *prior* sales**, and beware fuzzy-match false positives (same building + same area value may be a different identical-layout unit — that's actually still a good comp, but label it as approximate). Never let a *later* sale of the unit inform an earlier row.
- **Point-in-time integrity for slowly-updated joins.** Mollak service charges, building metadata, and project completion % are published as *current* values; joining today's value to a 22-month-old transaction is mild look-ahead. Acceptable for near-static fields (floors, developer), risky for completion %/pipeline — snapshot those monthly going forward.
- **Macro features cannot be validated by the existing CV alone.** With 24 months and 10 date-ordered folds, a macro series has ~2 dozen effective observations; the Sberbank lesson is that macro relationships break exactly at regime changes (their winner abandoned macro modeling for constant scaling factors). If added, restrict macro to the trend component, check fold-by-fold stability, and expect the optimizer's MedAPE gate to reject most of them.
- **Listing data (when it comes) has selection bias**: only marketed inventory, asking ≠ closing, and stale/duplicate listings; validate the sale-to-list ratio distribution per segment before trusting it.
- **Units/buildings joins will be partial** (~18% of transactions lack project name; villas lack building names — fine, we're apartments-only). Add explicit "metadata missing" indicators rather than imputing silently, and confirm the model's gain isn't concentrated only in matched rows before shipping.
