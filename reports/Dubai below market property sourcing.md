# An accurate model finds bargains already bought

The fair-value model is a sound pricing engine but not, by itself, the right approach to buying below market, and median absolute percentage error grades only the pricing. On that narrow test it meets the industry standard: no regulator sets a number, leading providers publish median errors of about 2% when they know a home's asking price and 6.2–7.7% when they do not, and the project's own measurement for ready apartments (6.03%, with 69.1% of estimates within 10%) matches the best provider figure for comparable stock, while the 4.15% headline is flattered by off-plan sales priced from developer lists. The goal needs two further jobs, detecting a real discount and sourcing one that can still be bought, and there the approach falls short: the model scores registered sales, which are deals another buyer has already closed, and its flags sit in the error tail that a median is built to ignore. By the project's own measurements 4.99% of held-out sales, and 7.7% of ready sales, already sit 15% or more below model value, whereas measured discounts from urgent private sellers run about 3–12% and Dubai court auctions open at the official market valuation and, in every reported result found, cleared above their reserve. The flagged gaps are real in one sense, since flagged units resold at model value and beat their district by 14–18% before costs, but sales recorded 15% above model value reverted too, no ground truth shows the cheap sales were distressed, and nothing shows an outsider could have bought at those prices. The remedy is to keep the model and build the missing layers around it: score live offers instead of closed sales, replace the fixed −15% line with a per-unit lower bound whose coverage is verified, grade the screen by the precision of its flags against labelled forced sales and by discount net of Dubai's 6–7% buying costs, and take evidence of seller pressure from legal and contractual deadlines, which a price gap cannot supply.

## Without an asking price, 6–8% median error is the benchmark the model meets

There is no single industry-standard error and no legal pass mark. The US six-agency rule on quality control for automated valuation models (AVMs), effective 1 October 2025, requires controls that "ensure a high level of confidence" in estimates and deliberately sets no prescriptive standard ([Federal Register, 7 Aug 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-07/pdf/2024-16197.pdf)); the European Banking Authority asks only that models be "valid and accurate" and backtested against transaction prices ([EBA/GL/2020/06](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library/Publications/Guidelines/2020/Guidelines%20on%20loan%20origination%20and%20monitoring/884283/EBA%20GL%202020%2006%20Final%20Report%20on%20GL%20on%20loan%20origination%20and%20monitoring.pdf)); the European AVM Alliance prescribes blind, out-of-sample testing reported by segment ([EAA Standards, 3rd ed.](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)). The numbers that exist are voluntary. The ratio-study standard of the International Association of Assessing Officers (IAAO), adopted by its AVM standard, asks for a median ratio of 0.90–1.10, a coefficient of dispersion of **5.0–10.0 for newer or homogeneous residential stock including condominiums**, a price-related differential of 0.98–1.03 and a price-related bias within ±0.05 ([IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies, 2013](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf); [IAAO Standard on AVMs, 2018](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)). Freddie Mac's product tiers rate an estimate high-confidence when its forecast standard deviation is 13% or less ([Freddie Mac](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheets/dougwhitepaper_metricsmatter.pdf)). One vendor proposes at least 80% of estimates within 10% when the list price is known and at least 60% when it is not ([HouseCanary, 2022](https://www.housecanary.com/images/65b19f5d2af745c8a24e1f7c_AE-AVM_Standards_Whitepaper_min-1.pdf)), and the US mortgage industry's common confidence score, final since April 2026, is defined on the same ±10% band ([AVMetrics, Apr 2026](https://www.avmetrics.net/2026/04/04/avm-confidence-score-calibration/)).

Published error rates divide on one question: did the estimate know the asking price? Redfin reports a median error of **1.94% for homes on the market and 7.66% for homes off it**; Zillow, as quoted by Boston.com, 1.77% and 7.20%; HouseCanary 2.5% once a home is listed and 6.8% before ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/redfin-estimate); [Boston.com, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.boston.com/real-estate/home-buying/2026/10/21/home-value-estimates-zillow-redfin/); [HouseCanary](https://www.housecanary.com/accuracy/)). HouseCanary says so directly: "The purchase figure is list-price informed. That's why it's lower." The independent US tester AVMetrics calls list-informed results "inflated performance metrics that don't reflect true, unassisted, predictive ability" and in May 2026 completed its move to testing only estimates produced before a home is listed ([AVMetrics, Aug 2025](https://www.avmetrics.net/2025/08/13/avm-testing-methodology-debate-avmetrics-response/); [AVMetrics, May 2026](https://www.avmetrics.net/2026/05/04/predictive-testing-methodology-replaces-legacy-avm-testing/)). **The industry standard is therefore about 2% with a listing price and about 7% without**, and a model that uses no asking prices is judged against the second figure.

Read against the no-listing rows, the project's own measurements put the model at the benchmark, not beyond it. **Ready apartments, at 6.03% median error with 69.1% within 10% and 90.4% within 20%, are level with HouseCanary's pre-listing condominium figure (6.2%, 68.1%)**, ahead of Redfin's and Zillow's off-market medians, and slightly behind the best urban academic results. The overall 4.15% beats every published no-listing figure found, but 69% of the held-out sales are off-plan, scored at 3.41%. Developers sell off-plan units from price lists, so a trailing median for the same project and unit type carries much of what an asking price carries elsewhere, and that figure belongs nearer the list-informed rows. The honest headline is the pair, 6.0% ready and 3.4% off-plan, not the blend. Three caveats travel with any such comparison. The US figures span every dwelling type nationwide, whereas repetitive tower apartments are among the easier stock to value (HouseCanary's own pre-listing figures are 4.9% for townhouses, 6.2% for condominiums and 6.9% for detached houses). Every vendor figure is self-reported. And vendors publish coverage beside accuracy, because accuracy "cannot be viewed independently from Coverage" ([EAA Standards, 3rd ed.](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)); the share of apartment sales the model can score belongs in the same table.

On the assessors' measures the model sits inside every range: a median ratio of 1.000, a coefficient of dispersion of 6.80 overall (8.83 ready, 5.88 off-plan), a price-related differential of 1.023 and a price-related bias of −0.011. One reading deserves a watch: the ready-apartment differential of 1.028 is close to the 1.03 ceiling above which the IAAO reads regressivity, and a model that overvalues cheaper units relative to dearer ones concentrates spurious "below value" flags at the cheap end. These ranges were written for tax assessors, assume trimmed outliers and are advisory. Converted to a forecast standard deviation under a normal-error assumption (this report's arithmetic), the shares within 10% and 20% imply about 8–11% overall and 10–12% for ready apartments, inside Freddie Mac's 13% high-confidence tier. The bundle the live app serves, trained on 10 August 2026, scores the same window at 4.32% overall and 6.21% on ready apartments and earns the same verdict. Ten-fold time-ordered cross-validation over 303,183 sales gives 3.98% with a standard deviation of 0.44 across folds, so the headline moves by several tenths of a point from one period to the next and no comparison should lean on its second decimal.

Dubai itself offers nothing to compare with. No Dubai valuation product publishes a defined, testable statistic: the only figures found are an undefined "above 97 per cent" for Bayut's TruEstimate in sponsored coverage and an undefined "AI prediction accuracy to 85%" for the Dubai Land Department's (DLD) own valuation project ([Gulf News, Nov 2025](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/why-half-a-million-truestimate-reports-signal-a-new-era-in-uae-real-estate-1.500357774); [Real Estate Asia](https://realestateasia.com/co-written-partner/event-news/dubai-land-department-revolutionises-ai-based-smart-property-valuation-system)). Published academic work on DLD transactions is weaker than the project's: a mean absolute percentage error of 11% on more than 700,000 transactions, and 25–36% for standard learners on one-bedroom sales, against the project's mean of 6.80% ([Alhammadi & Ibrahim, 2025](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/machine-learningbased-property-valuation-for-dubai-design-impleme/); [Balila & Shabri, 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fams.2024.1327376)). By reporting median error, the shares within 10% and 20%, dispersion and a segment split on a time-ordered hold-out, the project already discloses more than any Dubai AVM found.

How much lower can the error go? Not much. Valuation research treats a sale price as market value plus transaction noise with a standard deviation "around 7%" that "cannot be modeled away" ([Francke, 2024](https://iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/20241204-IAAO-Francke.pdf)); under normality that is a median-error floor near 4.7% for negotiated sales (this report's arithmetic on Dutch and US evidence, so indicative only). A ready-apartment error of 6.0% is close to that floor, which squares with the project's five improvement campaigns: the remaining error is unit-level, and rent and renovation-permit features moved it by 0.02 points or less. What closes the gap elsewhere, from about 7% to about 2%, is the listing itself.

## A 4% median cannot grade a screen that lives in the tail

Median absolute percentage error is a legitimate headline: Zillow, Redfin and HouseCanary lead with it, and the IAAO's 2026 draft says the typical percentage error "is expressed by the median absolute percentage error statistic" ([IAAO exposure draft, May 2026](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/StandardonRatioStudies_Exposure-Mar2026.pdf)). The same literature insists it cannot stand alone. Clear Capital's testing glossary notes that the median "hides the times the AVM was really far off" ([Clear Capital](https://www.clearcapital.com/blog-avm-testing-glossary/)); Steurer, Hill and Pfeifer show across 48 metrics that the choice of metric changes which model ranks best ([Steurer, Hill & Pfeifer, 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jpropr/v38y2021i2p99-129.html)); and the European standard's minimum report is an error histogram plus the shares within 10%, 15% and 20% for each property type, area and price band ([EAA Standards, 3rd ed.](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/files/eaa/Downloads/EAA_Standards_3rd_Edition.pdf)).

For a bargain screen the mismatch is structural. A median describes the middle of the error distribution and is prized because it ignores outliers; a flag list is made of outliers. The project's own measurements show how little the centre says about the tail: a normal error distribution with a 4.15% median would put the 90th-percentile error near 10% and well under 1% of sales 15% below model value, and the measured figures are **15.6% and 4.99%**. The −15% line is, in effect, the model's own fifth percentile. It is also a different statistical event in each segment. Off-plan sales cross it 3.8% of the time (495 of 13,105) and ready sales 7.7% (452 of 5,884), so ready apartments, 31% of sales, supply 48% of the flags; interpolating the project's figures, a line with the same one-in-twenty chance rate for ready apartments would sit near −18%. The valuation literature predicts the pattern: AVM performance "degrades with increasingly heterogenous assets or assets that are thinly traded" ([RICS, 2022](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/april_2022_automated_valuation_models_insight.pdf)).

Then comes the base rate. The share of flags that are true deep discounts equals the share of all sales that are true deep discounts, times the fraction of those the screen catches, divided by the 4.99% of sales flagged. If 1% of sales are genuine forced or urgent sales at 15% or more below market and the screen catches half of them, one flag in ten is real; if 2% are and it catches four in five, one in three. Half is a fair guess near the line, because error pushes a sale that is exactly 15% below market back across it as often as forward. **The true base rate in Dubai is unknown, and the clues point low**: court-auction lots equal to under 0.2% of a month's residential deals, a portal chief executive's 0.6% for distressed listings in the 2015–16 slowdown, and a brokerage calling foreclosure-driven supply "a relatively small portion of overall market activity" ([Emirates Auction](https://www.emiratesauction.com/properties); [Khaleej Times, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-residential-values-post-first-monthly-gain-since-february); [The National, 2016](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/property-agents-bait-dubai-homebuyers-with-distress-deals-1.177373); [Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/distressed-properties-dubai)). The general result comes from intrusion detection: when the target is rare, the false-alarm rate "is the limiting factor", and precision has to be read against the base rate ([Axelsson, 2000](https://www.profsandhu.com/cs6393_s20/p186-axelsson.pdf); [Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432)). The project's own label already concedes the point: of 415 sales below the line in the month to 12 November 2026, 22 carried a corroborating signal.

Professional AVMs do not issue bare numbers. Each estimate carries a forecast standard deviation, a confidence score or a value range, and the European label requires "a predictive measure of the estimated accuracy of each individual valuation" ([European AVM Alliance](https://www.europeanavmalliance.org/en/eaa-avm-label-accreditation/key-requirements.html)). The research standard is a prediction interval whose coverage is checked on held-out sales, and it is unforgiving. Intervals taken straight from quantile regression on boosted trees under-cover, 78% actual at 90% nominal in a San Francisco Bay Area study, while conformal calibration hit nominal coverage exactly ([Bastos & Paquette, 2024](https://rem.rc.iseg.ulisboa.pt/wps/pdf/REM_WP_0314_2024.pdf)). For Oslo apartments, a boosted-tree model with 71.0% of estimates within 10% and 93.8% within 20% (the project's overall figures are 78.8% and 93.8%) still needed **a valid 90% interval about 32% of the sale price wide**, and plain calibration left intervals "too large in certain geographical regions and too small in others" until it was weighted by location ([Hjort et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.06531v2)). A model in that accuracy class cannot separate a −15% sale from ordinary dispersion on price alone.

Ranking compounds the problem. Choosing the largest gaps from noisy estimates guarantees disappointment even when every estimate is unbiased (with ten equal candidates the expected shortfall is 1.5 standard deviations of the estimate), and the remedy is to shrink each gap toward zero in proportion to its uncertainty ([Smith & Winkler, 2006](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/2006-smith.pdf)). The scorecard for the screen follows: precision of the flag, and of the top-ranked flags, against a stated base rate; the false-flag rate by segment, which should be flat; coverage and interval score of the lower bound on held-out sales ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)); and outcome in money after costs. Median error, with the shares within 10% and 20%, the 90th percentile and the ready and off-plan split, stays as the health check of the valuer. One caution from finance applies to all of it: thresholds and signals tuned repeatedly on the same history inflate apparent performance, so the final test needs a period never used for any choice ([Bailey et al., 2014](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)).

## Urgency discounts run 3–12%, and the backtest cannot tell distress from noise

The measured size of real discounts is the second reason a −15% line misleads.

Two regimes emerge. A private seller under time pressure who still controls the sale concedes **roughly 3–12%**, and the concession is deadline-shaped: Danish estate sales cleared at market prices soon after the death and 14% below in the last three months before the legal deadline. That range is out of reach of a price screen, because by the project's own measurements 15.0% of ready sales already sit 10% or more below model value. Discounts of 15% or more belong to sellers who have lost control of the process to a court or a lender, or who sell to a cash buyer within weeks, and even those shrink under scrutiny: US foreclosure discounts fall from 10–28% to about 5–8% once condition and unobserved quality are controlled, and in Cape Town a raw auction discount of 56% fell to 14.5% after the buyer's commission, inherited arrears and eviction costs ([Davids, 2025](https://allandav.github.io/papers/Davids_Foreclosure_Discounts.pdf)). Apartments escape the neglected-maintenance part of such discounts, which is "very small for condominiums", but not the forced-sale part, which is larger for condominiums than for houses ([Campbell, Giglio & Pathak](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14866/w14866.pdf)). No academic or official measurement was found for Dubai; the local evidence is press and broker commentary, measured against asking prices, purchase prices or pre-conflict values and never against matched sales.

The project's walk-forward backtest goes further than the published literature, in which no study was found that takes a model residual as an advance buy rule and tests it on realised returns. Its result is real and bounded. Units bought at −15% to −35% entered at a median −19.8% and resold at +3.2%, so the low price was not a lasting defect of the unit; flagged buyers beat their district by a median 18.2% over holds of 30 to 183 days (controls −0.3%), and by 14.2% (95% interval 10.5 to 23.0, n = 60) on registry-proven identical apartments. Three things it does not show. The first is cause. The project's own validation report records that sales 15% or more above model value also resold at model value (+21.6% at entry, +0.4% on resale), and reversion in both directions is what a one-off transaction shock predicts. The housing literature finds exactly that: the house-specific part of a sale price is large, does not grow with the holding period and behaves as a single shock "realized at the time of sale" ([Giacoletti, 2017](https://finance.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/MarcoGiacolettiJMP20170116082355.pdf)), and in Berlin the foreclosure discount "is likely restricted to the transaction itself" ([Daminger & Wiersma, 2024](https://wifo.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/upload-8272/wp_2024_675_.pdf)). Urgency, bargaining luck, a price that was never a market price and work done between the two sales all produce the same picture. The second is access. The buyers the literature shows purchasing far below market are repeat professionals: in Los Angeles **high-volume middlemen bought 31% below expected value while low-volume flippers bought 6% below and drew 84% of their return from market growth** ([Bayer et al., 2020](https://sites.duke.edu/patrickbayer/files/2021/02/BGMR_RFS.pdf)). The project's flagged units resold more often than controls (27.3% against 21.5%), the signature of that trade; the backtest measures what those buyers captured, not what an outsider was offered. The third is money: the returns are before costs, and the main US study of bank-owned homes found the implied discount "less than typical transaction costs" ([Harding, Rosenblatt & Yao, 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/juecon/v71y2012i2p204-218.html)).

Assessment standards add the reading the registry cannot rule out: the price was never a market price. The IAAO's sales-verification standard lists the transfers that fail the arm's-length test (sales between relatives or affiliates, lender sales, estate settlements, court-ordered sales, partial interests, bulk allocations) and places them at the low end of the price range. It endorses an extreme ratio as a prompt to verify and insists that sales "should never be excluded from a ratio study solely on the basis of the computed ratio" ([IAAO, 2020](https://iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Verification_Adjustment_of_Sales.pdf)). A land registry is the unverified layer of that process, so a screen for low prices is first of all a detector of sales that need checking. The project's backtest agrees at the extreme: gaps beyond −35% behaved as data noise, and 79 of the 947 held-out flags were that deep. In Dubai a transfer to a first-degree relative can be registered as a gift for 0.125% instead of the 4% sale fee, which gives most family transfers a reason to appear as gifts, but a cheap "sale" between related parties remains possible and looks like a bargain ([Khaleej Times, 2022](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/dubai-do-i-need-to-pay-a-fee-to-transfer-my-property-to-family-members)).

This is why the "distressed" label needs rebuilding, not tuning. With no forced-sale vocabulary in the DLD procedure names (zero across 141,749 out-of-sample scored sales), **the label rests on two proxies, and the literature supports neither as a marker of seller pressure**. A project with fewer than 8 sales is the definition of thin comparables, the condition under which valuation error is widest, so that signal travels with the model's error instead of standing apart from it. And the one study located that measured seller count, on Massachusetts estate and bankruptcy sales, reports discounts roughly twice as large with a single seller as with two ([Campbell, Giglio & Pathak](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14866/w14866.pdf)). The signals with measured effects are legal and contractual: a foreclosure notice (9–12% below comparables in Cape Town even when the sale happened outside the auction), a statutory deadline (14% in Denmark's final three months) and a failed auction (2.6% lower on the later sale in Australia) ([Davids, 2025](https://allandav.github.io/papers/Davids_Foreclosure_Discounts.pdf); [Andersen & Nielsen](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2017/08/when-forced-sales-turn-fire-sales); [Cortés et al., 2022](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30760)). The intuitive signal runs backwards. Owners with high loan-to-value ratios or paper losses ask more, wait longer and sell for more while they control the sale ([Genesove & Mayer, 1997](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4861); [Genesove & Mayer, 2001](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8143)), and "motivated" in a listing marks a home that sold 4–8% lower after taking 15% longer, a lagging sign of earlier overpricing ([Los Angeles Times via The Spokesman-Review, 2006](https://spokesman.com/stories/2006/dec/17/motivated-seller-just-dont-say-so)).

## Dubai's forced-sale channels are narrow, capped and invisible in the registry

The one true forced-sale channel is the court execution sale. Under Dubai's mortgage law a lender gives 30 days' notarised notice and then asks the execution judge to attach the property "so that it can be sold by public auction"; a clause letting the lender take or sell the unit outside that process is void ([Law No. 14 of 2008](https://www.visitdubai.com/-/media/Files/invest-in-dubai/corporate-legislation-docs/domestic-economic-legislation/en/court-mortgage-law-no-14-of-2008-of-the-emirate-of-dubai)). The mechanics cap the discount. By a Dubai law firm's account, the court's expert, usually the DLD, values the property "according to the current market price"; **the first session cannot clear below that valuation plus the lender's costs, each failed session lowers the floor by 5% to a maximum of 25%**, and only after a further three-month postponement can the unit sell for as little as half the valuation ([BSA Law, 2024](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/the-process-of-selling-a-mortgaged-property-through-a-court-supervised-auction-in-dubai/)). Bidders lodge a 20% deposit by manager's cheque or transfer, pay the balance within 10 days or forfeit it, and can view a unit only if it is vacant and the court has handed over the key ([Emirates Auction, deposits](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/11); [Emirates Auction, property](https://www.emiratesauction.com/faqs/9)). It is a cash channel in a market where a mortgage approval can take up to eight weeks ([MyBayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/distressed-properties-dubai/)).

Volume is tiny and the price record runs against the bargain hunter. On 15 November 2026 Emirates Auction listed **37 property lots, 17 of them in Dubai, against roughly 10,600 residential deals in October** ([Emirates Auction](https://www.emiratesauction.com/properties); [Khaleej Times, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-residential-values-post-first-monthly-gain-since-february)). Every documented outcome found, all from 2011–2014, cleared above the reserve: bidders paid "5% to 40% higher than the reserve price" in 2012, and Dubai Courts' 2014 e-auctions of seized assets, cars included, raised AED 365.6 million against valuations of AED 283 million ([Hadef & Partners, 2012](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/inside-the-dubai-land-departments-property-auctions/); [The National, 2014](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/dubai-courts-earns-dh365m-from-auctioning-seized-assets-1.568273)). Marketing guides claim auction purchases "historically closed about 10–30% below open-market value" ([Real Estate Club Dubai, 2026](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/investment/dubai-property-auctions-2026-emirates-auction-bank-repossessions-guide)), but no transaction-based study or official series of sell-through and price against valuation was found to support it. Wide participation works against deep discounts: opening Florida's foreclosure auctions to online bidding cut the discount by 3 points ([Mazzola, 2023](https://www.suerf.org/suerf-policy-brief/64227/technology-and-asset-liquidations-evidence-from-real-estate-collateral)), and Dubai's auctions are online and open at market value. The buyer's structural gain is the registration fee, 2% to the DLD instead of the usual 4%; the structural risk is a valuation certificate up to six months old in a market whose index fell 10.2% between February and August 2026 ([DLD auction registration](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/); [Khaleej Times, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/6-in-10-dubai-homes-hold-their-value-as-market-cools-gently-in-august)). Checking a reserve against today's value is exactly what the model is for.

The larger pool of pressured sellers is off-plan, 72% of transactions in the first quarter of 2026 ([Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.ae/site-assets/pdf/2026/dubai-residential-market-review-q1-2026.pdf)). Article 11 of the interim-register law, as replaced in 2020, lets a developer act against a defaulting buyer through the DLD without a court: after a 30-day notice it can terminate and keep **up to 40% of the unit's contract value** once the building is 60% complete (25% below that), or above 80% completion ask the DLD to auction the unit ([Law No. 19 of 2020](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2020/Law%20No.%20%2819%29%20of%202020%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2813%29%20of%202008%20Regulating%20the%20Interim%20Real%20Property%20Register%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html); [Khaleej Times, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/missed-off-plan-payments-dubai-developer-legally-do)). Terminated units return to the developer, and developers are not discounting: only 44 of 717 projects launched since July 2023 cut prices by 5% or more between the end of February and late September 2026 ([Khaleej Times, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-developers-hold-off-plan-pricing-as-only-6-projects-cut-prices-by-5-or-more-since-february)). The bargain, where one exists, is the step before default. A buyer facing forfeiture of up to 40% has every reason to sell the contract at a loss, and one broker reports secondary off-plan apartments trading "10–15% below original values" ([Fortune, 1 Jun 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/property-prices-down-dubai/)). That phrase is the trap: "below original price" compares the asking price with what the seller paid, not with today's market, and only a current valuation settles which it is.

Private distress is owner-driven and mostly off-market. A Dubai brokerage describes it as "typically owner driven urgent sales not widespread foreclosure events", often traded through broker networks before reaching portals, with discounts "often marginal rather than dramatic" in a strong market ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/distressed-properties-dubai)). The "distress" label on portals is unverified marketing: in the 2015–16 slowdown agents advertised it on listings whose sellers wanted market price, and a broker put a true distress sale at about AED 500,000 off a AED 3 million value, or 17% ([The National, 2016](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/property-agents-bait-dubai-homebuyers-with-distress-deals-1.177373)). Portal terms forbid scraping or compiling listing databases, and Property Finder's API is limited to agencies and approved partners, so asking prices have to be read by hand, obtained from brokers or licensed ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.com/ae/terms-and-conditions/); [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/terms.html); [Dubizzle](https://www.dubizzle.com/terms/)). The registry marks none of this. The DLD registers auction sales through a separate service, so it knows which transfers were auctions, but its open transactions data carry no such label, which is consistent with the project's own finding; the same open data do include mortgage and gift registrations, a valuations table and a projects table with statuses such as "Freezed" and "Under Cancellation Decision" ([DLD auction registration](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-registration-of-a-property-sold-in-an-auction/); [DLD open data](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/open-data/real-estate-data)).

Costs set the hurdle. A resale buyer pays the 4% DLD fee, about 2.1% agent commission with VAT and a trustee fee, which is about 6.4% on a AED 1.5 million cash purchase by this report's arithmetic and 7–10% all-in on Bayut's estimate ([DLD fee list](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/registering-the-sale-of-a-mortgaged-property/); [MyBayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/cost-buying-property-dubai/)). **After those costs a 10% gross discount leaves 3–4% of equity and a 15% discount 8–9%**; a buyer who means to resell needs 8–9% gross to break even, and the project's measured 14–18% excess return shrinks to roughly 6–10% after a round trip. Only a buyer who would pay the same costs on any purchase keeps the whole discount against the alternative. Three legal frictions also lower a price without any distress. A tenant's rights survive the sale and vacant possession takes 12 months' formal notice ([Law No. 33 of 2008](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2009/Law%20No.%20%2833%29%20of%202008%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2826%29%20of%202007.html); [Khaleej Times, 20 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/can-landlord-dubai-evict-sell-property-uae-law)). Unpaid service charges are a lien, and the unit "may not be disposed of unless these charges are paid" ([Law No. 6 of 2019](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2019/Law%20No.%20%286%29%20of%202019%20Concerning%20Ownership%20of%20Jointly%20Owned%20Real%20Property%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)). A mortgaged unit transfers only after the buyer's cheque has cleared the seller's bank debt and the bank has released the mortgage ([DLD fee list](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/registering-the-sale-of-a-mortgaged-property/)). A model that cannot see occupancy, arrears or encumbrance reads each of these as a discount.

Timing favours preparation over haste. The ValuStrat index fell 10.2% between February and August 2026 after a regional conflict began, then rose 0.1% in October, its first gain since February ([Khaleej Times, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/6-in-10-dubai-homes-hold-their-value-as-market-cools-gently-in-august); [Khaleej Times, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-residential-values-post-first-monthly-gain-since-february)). Ready deals in the first half of 2026 were 26% below a year earlier and sellers have mostly held ([REIDIN, Jul 2026](https://reidin.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/4.-Dubai-Off-Plan-vs-Ready-Report.pdf); [Khaleej Times, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-property-buyers-hunt-distressed-deals-but-sellers-hold-firm-on-prices)). The buffers are temporary: banks deferred AED 13.5 billion of repayments for more than 135,000 customers, the first deferrals began to be reclassified in September 2026, and analysts expect a lag of six to twelve months before the true picture shows ([EnterpriseAM, 22 Sep 2026](https://enterpriseam.com/uae/2026/09/22/cbuaes-wartime-deferral-shield-starts-coming-off-uae-bank-balance-sheets-this-month/)), while S&P expects 2027–2028 deliveries to press hardest on "investor-driven apartment segments" ([GDN Online, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.gdnonline.com/Details/1408949/SP-assesses-impact-of-Middle-East-war-on-Dubai-property-market)). On that calendar, and this is an inference, mortgage enforcement rises through 2027, with the statute's notice, valuation and auction steps adding months, and off-plan handover pressure arrives sooner.

## Source first, value second: six changes

Professionals find the seller first and value the property second. The documented discounts go to middlemen with a pipeline to motivated sellers, to "traditional cash buyer intermediaries" who "target distressed properties, such as foreclosures", and to agents who know the available listings and previous purchase prices ([Bayer et al., 2020](https://sites.duke.edu/patrickbayer/files/2021/02/BGMR_RFS.pdf); [Buchak et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28252/w28252.pdf); [Agarwal et al., 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v134y2019i3p715-735.html)). In that workflow a valuation model is the triage and pricing tool that follows sourcing. The model does not need replacing; six things around it need to change.

| Element | Today | Change |
|---|---|---|
| What is scored | Registered sales, which are closed deals | Live offers: court-auction lots, broker and portal asking prices, off-plan resale offers |
| Flag rule | Fixed −15% for every unit | Price below a calibrated per-unit lower bound; ranked gaps shrunk by their uncertainty |
| Ground truth | None | About 100 Emirates Auction results matched to registered sales; a hold-out period never used for tuning |
| Evidence of pressure | Thin project or more than one seller | Auction lot, lender or court in the process, dated deadline, price-cut history; thin projects treated as a caution |
| Scorecard | Median error, 4.15% | Flag precision against labels and a stated base rate, false-flag rate by segment, lower-bound coverage, discount net of 6–7% costs; median error kept as the valuer's health check, split into ready and off-plan |
| Model age | Served bundle trained on 10 August 2026 | Weekly retraining |

### Score offers, not history

The model answers one question well, what a unit is worth today, and the literature supports putting that question to a property on offer now. Comparing an asking price with a sales-based value is sound; building the value model on asking prices is not, since in Berlin an ask-based model was biased upward and less accurate, and "ask data are no replacement for sale data" ([Kolbe et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7805573)). In Dubai the two cannot be compared in aggregate, because average listings ran 52.67% above average transacted values at the May 2023 peak as different properties are listed and sold ([Global Capital Partners with Reidin, 2024](https://reidin.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Dubai-Residential-Mind-the-Gap-Evaluating-the-Difference-Between-Listings-and-Transactions.pdf)); the match has to be made unit by unit. The practical order is the handful of Dubai auction lots listed at any time, scored against their court valuation; deals circulated by brokers; hand-entered or licensed asking prices with their price-cut history; and off-plan resale offers scored against today's value instead of original price. The registry flags keep a secondary role as a map of where gaps recur.

Scoring offers brings a hazard the registry screen never faced: adverse selection. Zillow closed its home-buying arm in 2021 after an inventory write-down of about $304 million, having converted "only about 10% of the serious sellers" who asked for an offer ([Zillow 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764021000085/q32021991.htm); [Zillow shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764021000085/exhibit993.htm)). The academic account is that an owner who could sell quickly elsewhere "only sells their home to an iBuyer when algorithmic error overvalues their home", and that algorithmic buyers earn less on the hard-to-price homes they do buy ([Buchak et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28252/w28252.pdf)). The mirror image applies to a bargain hunter: the offer furthest below one's own model value is the offer on which the model is most likely too high, or on which the seller knows something the data do not. The survivors' answer is to act only where model error is small, and an Oslo simulation points the same way, with screening on the model's uncertainty, not on the size of the gap, raising simulated margins ([Pollestad & Oust, 2025](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:25:y:2025:i:11:p:1771-1788)). For this model that means trusting a gap in a building with dense recent comparables and treating the same gap in a thin project as a reason to inspect.

### Flag on a verified lower bound, then get labels

The fixed −15% line should give way to a per-unit lower bound, for example the fifth percentile of a conformal interval calibrated on recent out-of-sample scores by segment or building cluster, so that the chance of a false flag is the same for a ready flat in a thin project as for an off-plan unit in a dense one. Weekly retraining already produces the out-of-sample residuals this needs. Ranked gaps should be shrunk toward zero in proportion to that uncertainty. Labels come next, and the project has already identified the route: **match about 100 Emirates Auction results to registered sales** by area, size, price and date. Those labels turn "distressed" from an assertion into a measured precision and recall, and they reveal what an auction achieves against model value, the number no Dubai source publishes. A 2019 independent test of a US seller-propensity score shows the form honest reporting takes: 19.5% of flagged owners sold within six months, against about 1% for cold calling, while the vendor's own customers claimed 36.4% ([Inman, 2019](https://www.inman.com/2019/03/26/revaluates-likely-to-move-score-is-right-1-in-5-times/)).

### Take the evidence of pressure from deadlines

Evidence of seller pressure has to come from data that do not depend on price. Ranked by the strength of the evidence, the order is a legal or contractual deadline with a known date (an auction session, an Article 11 notice, a handover instalment); a lender or court already in the process; a listing with repeated cuts after a high start; vacancy with an institutional or estate seller; and, last, listing keywords. The project already holds useful raw material. Mortgage registrations identify leveraged units, which is a watch list and not a trigger, since leverage predicts a low price only once a lender or court takes over. Gift registrations help separate family transfers. The DLD projects table locates developments where Article 11 pressure concentrates. Rent contracts are the natural source for an occupancy flag wherever they can be tied to a unit, and building permits can indicate whether work was done between a flagged purchase and its resale. Forced sales, once labelled, belong outside the model's training data but inside the database with a tag, as the standards prescribe: confirmed forced sales "would generally be excluded" from market-value evidence ([RICS Red Book, 2025](https://rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/Red-Book-Global-Standards-incorporating-IVS.pdf)), and the tag, not the residual, is the evidence of a channel. Freshness completes the list. By the project's own measurement a four-month-old model overstated values by about 1.1% and raised the flag rate by about 2 points in a softening market, and a softening market is when distressed supply appears.

## Conclusion

The step back moves the constraint. Rent and renovation-permit features shifted the median by 0.02 points or less because the model already sits near the transaction-noise floor; a hundred auction labels and a weekly sheet of scored offers would change what the project knows by far more than a sixth campaign. Valuation is also becoming a commodity in Dubai. Bayut says the share of secondary ready transactions backed by one of its automated valuation reports rose from 6% to 55% in a year ([Gulf News, Sep 2025](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/truestimate-valuations-now-power-half-of-uae-property-deals-bayut-confirms-1.500256508)), and where "most property transactions are still paid in cash" ([Khaleej Times, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-banks-resilient-despite-real-estate-growth-moodys-ratings)), cash is an entry ticket, not an edge. The scarce inputs are the two the project lacks: sight of offers before they close and proof of the seller's deadline.

The model's durable value is defensive. In a market that fell 10% in six months, a "distress" label, a court valuation set months earlier and a claim of "below original price" are all anchored to a stale price, and a current, sales-based value with an honest range is what tells a discount from the index. The realistic prize is a gross discount of 5–15% from a seller facing a dated deadline, of which Dubai's buying costs take 6–7 points; deeper discounts are documented only for buyers who operate repeatedly and at scale. The forbearance calendar places the larger supply of such sellers in 2027, which leaves time to build the pipeline and the labels before it arrives.
